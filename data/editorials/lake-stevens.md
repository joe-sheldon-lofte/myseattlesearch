### **EP1**

Long before the town took its current shape, this shoreline was a seasonal stop for the Skykomish and Snohomish, known in Lushootseed as sduhubš. They gathered berries along the slopes and hunted the Pilchuck River basin until the 1855 Treaty of Point Elliott relocated tribal members to the Tulalip reservation at dxʷlilap. By 1889, white settlers gave the tentative settlement the name Outing, but everything shifted when Howard and Bethel Rucker arrived in 1905. The Rucker brothers redirected Cassidy Creek, ran a spur from the railroad line at Hartford, and opened a massive shingle mill in 1907 that drove the local economy until it burned down in 1925. Somewhere down in the murky depths of the lake sits a steam locomotive that plunged off a timber trestle in the early 1910s, rediscovered by Navy divers back in 1995 as a quiet reminder of that relentless logging era.

January brings a low fog that hangs over the water, muting the shoreline and wrapping Old Town in cold, quiet stillness. It is the best time of year to grab a hot latte and a warm scratch biscuit sandwich at Biscuit + Bean on Main Street before crossing over to North Cove Park. You can stroll along the wide, ADA accessible boardwalk with a steaming mug in hand, stepping into The Mill to explore the historical exhibits housed inside the modern civic building built directly over the old industrial site. From the elevated observation deck, you get an uninterrupted view across the gray water, watching early morning mist roll across the lake without competing with summer crowds or speedboats.

Living here in the winter means getting comfortable with two distinct sides of town. The commercial stretch along Highway 9 at Frontier Village is where you handle your high-volume errands, driving from one mega-parking lot to the next because nobody walks that corridor. Down in Old Town, the scale drops back down to human proportions, where rigid civic rules cap accessory buildings at a low twelve feet and strictly limit paved surfaces to protect the watershed. You quickly learn that if you need to bypass the gridlock near Highway 204 during peak hours, taking Vernon Road or turning onto 20th Street SE via 91st Avenue NE saves you from getting stuck in arterial standstill.

On these frigid mornings, the lake acts as a mirror for crisp winter skies. Watching the early light hit the water from a bench at North Cove gives you a sense of why people stay through the dark months. If you want a low-stimulus escape away from even the light foot traffic in Old Town, head over to the Catherine Creek Park forest trails or the quiet shoreline by Sunset Cove. The tall evergreens block the biting wind, leaving only the sound of dripping cedar needles and winter birds settling into the brush.
