### **EP1**

Back in 1889, developer F.A. Blasher and his associates named this spot Des Moines to entice Midwesterners to move west. They pitched grand visions while timber chutes slid raw logs straight into Puget Sound. That gritty logging origin still shows underneath the coastal paint. Long before Blasher sold his dream, Duwamish and Muckleshoot communities gathered along Des Moines Creek during seasonal salmon runs, understanding this coastline long before anyone drew grid maps.

Duck into Auntie Irene's Coffee Shop on a chilly morning for a hot drink, then head down to the 670-foot concrete public fishing pier. The salt mist hangs heavy over the water, and you can watch grey waves churn toward Vashon Island while gulls circle overhead.

Navigating town during the post-holiday reset is simple enough until you try to buy groceries on foot. While downtown Marine View Drive S is easy for walking to cafes and local eateries, real grocery shopping requires driving east to Pacific Highway S or north toward Normandy Park. If afternoon traffic on Kent-Des Moines Road locks up, cut west via 16th Ave S down through 223rd Street to reach the waterfront directly.

The post-holiday mood here is quiet and introspective. Walk the boardwalk at Des Moines Beach Park where the creek meets the estuary, or hide away with a good book at the Highline College Library on the upper bluff to watch low fog drag across the sound.
