### **EP1**

Shoreline received its name back in 1944 when the local school district was drawn to span shore to shore, connecting Puget Sound over to Lake Washington. Long before incorporation, this stretch was just a collection of quiet neighborhoods linked by the Seattle-Everett Interurban Railway trolley line. Today, that old electric rail bed lives on as the paved 14.8-mile Interurban Trail, reminding us that this town was built around simple transit connections rather than sleek master plans.

For an easy two-hour winter reset, swing by Rise & Shine Bakery on Highway 99 to grab a warm bag of baked treats or a savory banh mi. Drive west down to Richmond Beach Saltwater Park, where you can cross the footbridge over the BNSF railway tracks down to the cobble shoreline. Take a slow stroll along the tide line watching Puget Sound ferry traffic cut through the winter mist, then wrap up your walk on the bluff-top trail benches looking out across the gray water toward the Olympic Mountains.

Living here means understanding the stark contrast between the commercial strip along Highway 99 and the human scale of the interior streets. When traffic gridlocks on Aurora Avenue North between 145th and 185th, locals bypass the mess by taking Dayton Avenue North on the west side or 15th Avenue NE on the east. If you are catching a morning commute, keep in mind that the light rail parking lots at 148th and 185th fill up completely by 7:30 AM on weekdays, so getting an early start is essential.

January brings a quiet, reflective atmosphere as low saltwater fog settles over the valleys and beaches. The land carries deep Coast Salish roots, where the Suquamish, Duwamish, and Snohomish peoples once gathered seasonal harvests along Boeing Creek. On damp afternoons, Ronald Bog Park offers a peaceful sensory retreat, with its boardwalks wrapping around wetland bogs where blue herons rest among the reeds away from city noise.

### **EP2**

The Crest Theatre on 5th Avenue NE has been a suburban institution since it opened back in 1949. With its glowing neon marquee still lighting up the block, it offers a window into mid-century neighborhood life, much like the historic Highland Ice Arena, which trained local skaters for over six decades after the Lloyd family opened it in 1962. Shoreline has always excelled at preserving these quirky community anchors while quietly evolving around them.

When grey February skies take over, spend a cozy couple of hours grabbing a steaming bowl of noodle soup at Le's Pho Tai or soup dumplings at Xiaolongbao House along Aurora Avenue North. Afterward, skip the rain entirely by heading over to the Crest Theatre to catch an afternoon budget matinee. It is the ultimate low-stress escape when the weather refuses to cooperate.

Navigating daily errands here requires knowing which corridor suits your mood. Highway 99 is the major arterial spine for big-box errands and international grocery runs, while 15th Avenue NE in North City and 5th Avenue NE in Ridgecrest feel like walkable neighborhood villages. To bypass the inevitable I-5 backup near North 175th Street, use 5th Avenue NE as your steady north-south alternative.

February is built for seeking warmth along the international food strip that defines much of Aurora. Beyond the Asian dining options, you will find spots like Mediterranean Oasis and various Latin American taquerias that give the arterial its distinct flavor. On damp evenings, locals head to Darrell's Tavern to play vintage arcade games or catch live indie music in a wonderfully unpretentious dive bar environment.

### **EP3**

Shoreline honors its history in quiet, meaningful ways, from the preserved 1912 Ronald School building to Pratt Park, named in memory of civil rights leader Edwin T. Pratt. Local roots run deep here, shaped by notable figures like actor Rainn Wilson and soccer star Michelle Akers, who both attended Shorecrest High School. That grounded, suburban identity persists even as modern light rail developments reshape the landscape.

Early spring calls for a two-hour outing starting with a hot drink at Drumlin in the Ridgecrest neighborhood. From there, head west to Kruckeberg Botanic Garden to wander four acres filled with over two thousand rare native and exotic woody plants. Afterwards, cross 15th Avenue NW into Boeing Creek Park, following the dirt ravine path down to the shaded creek bed under massive second-growth Douglas firs.

To enjoy Kruckeberg at its best, plan a visit on Thursday or Friday afternoons for a quiet walk before weekend garden shoppers arrive. On the retail front, you do not need a walkable downtown to get things done, but you do need a strategy. Save time by hitting Town & Country Market on Westminster Way N before 9:00 AM on weekends so you can browse the aisles without bumping shopping carts.

March brings unpredictable spring sun squalls and emerging green buds across the municipal tree canopy. The season brings community gatherings like the Lantern Festival at Shoreline Community College. As daylight begins to stretch, the ancestral lands of the Coast Salish along Boeing Creek remind us of the long relationship between local waterways and seasonal change.

If you rely on the light rail at the 148th or 185th stations, remember that commuter spaces vanish quickly after 7:30 AM, so timing your morning travel is critical.

April brings daylight stretching past 7:30 PM and a fresh energy to local parks. ### **EP5**

That rich regional lore gives context to modern Shoreline, where quiet residential pockets sit just blocks away from busy commercial avenues.

July brings peak Pacific Northwest weather, with clear skies and warm temperatures tempered by Puget Sound ocean breezes.
