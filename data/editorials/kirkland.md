### **EP1**

January brings a quiet, low fog that drifts off Lake Washington and hangs across the waterfront. Back in 1888, British industrialist Peter Kirk envisioned turning this shore into a heavy industrial hub called the "Pittsburgh of the West" centered on the Great Western Iron and Steel Company. The Panic of 1893 flattened that dream before commercial iron ever flowed from the blast furnaces, forcing the settlement to pivot toward wool manufacturing, berry farming, and shipbuilding. That failed industrial empire left behind a compact human footprint rather than smokestacks.

A crisp winter morning is best started along Park Lane, a curbless shared street designed with permeable pavers so pedestrians and slow traffic share the brick space naturally. Grab a warm espresso at Kirkland Coffeeroasters Spotlight, then follow the shoreline promenade past Marina Park up to the broad slopes of Heritage Park. From the upper lawn, you get an uninterrupted view across gray water toward the Seattle skyline, with low clouds drifting over the lake.

Living here in midwinter requires a practical approach to daily errands. The downtown core is remarkably easy to walk for a morning coffee or a casual dinner, but running routine supermarket trips means jumping in the car to reach Totem Lake Town Center or heading up NE 85th St toward Rose Hill. When rain picks up and parking at Marina Park is full, locals duck into the free underground Peter Kirk Municipal Parking Garage beneath the library or park beneath Kirkland Urban to stay completely dry.

Standing on the Heritage Park bluff in the cold air offers a moment to reflect on the deeper roots of this shoreline. Long before industrial blast furnaces or modern brick plazas were built, these eastern banks served as traditional fishing and gathering grounds for the Duwamish, Suquamish, and Snoqualmie peoples. Watching low clouds lift off the lake provides a peaceful seasonal reset before the daily rhythm resumes.

### **EP2**

February is the month for ducking inside historic spaces and enjoying cold waterfront strolls. The Peter Kirk Building at Market Street and 7th Avenue stands as the oldest commercial brick building on the Eastside, erected in 1889 as Kirk's personal investment office. Saved from the wrecking ball in 1960, it now houses the non-profit Kirkland Arts Center, offering warm gallery spaces filled with local community artwork while winter rains fall outside.

A satisfying two-hour escape begins with a brisk walk along the cold boardwalks at Marina Park, watching ducks slice through the dark water. Afterward, head downtown for a warm table at Kirkland Fishnchips Spotlight for comfort food, or drive up toward Totem Lake to explore international dining options in the local multicultural corridors.

Navigating the town during late afternoon rush hour takes a bit of strategy. Heading toward I-405 on NE 85th St often comes to a crawl as regional traffic stacks up. Local drivers bypass that bottleneck by cutting south down Lake Washington Blvd NE to join the freeway at NE 70th St, or by taking 116th Ave NE as a parallel northerly route.

Long before the Seattle Seahawks established their original practice facility down at the Lake Washington Shipyard in 1976, local life revolved around quiet winter sanctuaries. On gray afternoons, residents find low-sensory comfort inside the Kirkland Library or wandering the quiet corridors of Kirkland Urban's covered retail plazas, far removed from highway noise.

### **EP3**

March brings dramatic spring weather, where blue skies suddenly turn to quick sun squalls over Lake Washington. Long before Costco co-founders Jim Sinegal and Jeffrey Brotman established their very first wholesale warehouse here in 1983, creating the worldwide "Kirkland Signature" brand, this shoreline was home to heavy timber and shipbuilding. At Carillon Point, the historic Lake Washington Shipyard built wooden ferries and warships during WWII before eventually transforming into a peaceful marina and hotel complex.

An ideal early spring outing starts at Juanita Beach Park. Walk along the sandy bay, cross the footbridge into Juanita Bay Park, and venture along the elevated wooden boardwalks. The wetlands are coming alive, making it a prime spot for birdwatching and spotting western painted turtles sunning themselves on log perches during unexpected breaks in the clouds.

Unpredictable March downpours mean knowing where to dodge the rain during daily errands. While boutique shops along Park Lane are fun to explore on foot, grabbing household supplies requires driving out to commercial plazas along NE 85th St. On wet afternoons, the covered walkways at Kirkland Urban offer a quick indoor pivot when outdoor walks get washed out.

Interpretive markers along the Juanita Bay boardwalks honor the Coast Salish heritage of the Duwamish and Snoqualmie peoples who managed these wetlands for generations. As daylight begins to stretch into the evening, catching the sunset from the benches at David E. Brink Park provides a front-row seat to wild spring clouds rolling over the water.

### **EP4**

Today, strict civic zoning limits building heights to three to five stories, keeping the skyline low and protecting public views of the lake.

For a great spring afternoon, grab a coffee at Praxis Yoga & Cafe or Kirkland Coffeeroasters Spotlight, then jump onto the Cross Kirkland Corridor. This flat, five-and-a-half-mile trail follows an old rail line, featuring a crushed-gravel and paved surface that welcomes walkers, strollers, adaptive bikes, and cyclists straight through the center of town.

Spring weather draws crowds back to the lakefront, making parking near Marina Park tricky on warm afternoons. Skip the circle around surface parking meters and park in the underground Peter Kirk Municipal Garage for easy access to shops. If you need to make a quick grocery run, heading toward Rose Hill keeps you away from central waterfront congestion.

Outdoor dining areas protected by public art and safety bollards begin filling up. People linger over street tacos from El Taco Loco or sit down for dinner at Always Thai Restaurant while daylight slowly fades over the lake.
