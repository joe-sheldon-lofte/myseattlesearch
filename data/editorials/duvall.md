### **EP1**

January in the valley brings a quiet reset as low mist crawls across the Snoqualmie River and settles over the floodplain. Long before timber workers laid down iron rails, the Snoqualmie Tribe and Tulalip Tribes traveled this river as a seasonal highway for fishing and gathering. Civil War veterans Francis and James Duvall homesteaded here in 1871, originally settling half a mile north at Cherry Valley. It was only in 1909, when the Chicago, Milwaukee & Puget Sound Railroad rolled through, that townspeople physically dragged their buildings up the hill to form today's Main Street corridor.

That same stubborn pioneer spirit still shapes daily life, especially when you need to run errands along SR 203 during the wet midwinter months. While central Main Street has a surprisingly high walkability score for a rural outpost, the moment you live up on the surrounding hillsides, your car keys are mandatory. Weekday evening commute traffic on Main Street bogs down fast, so smart residents skip the gridlock by ducking down 1st Ave NE or slipping across West Snoqualmie River Rd NE on the valley floor. When running into town, leave the Safeway spots for actual grocery shoppers and park in the free public gravel lot at Depot Park instead.

A perfect winter two hour outing starts by grabbing a hot drink at Duvall Coffeeroasters in the center of town. From there, take a slow stroll past historic storefronts down toward Depot Park and step onto the crushed gravel surface of the Snoqualmie Valley Trail. Following the path northbound before looping down to the sandy riverbank at McCormick Park offers total peacefulness, with cold valley air cutting through the bare cottonwood branches and zero crowds in sight.

If cold drizzle turns heavy, retreat indoors to warm up or seek out quieter spots like Sandlea Park for a low-stimulus breather away from traffic noise. The strict small-town architectural rules keep Main Street looking like a vintage settlement, while the surrounding Urban Growth Boundary prevents urban sprawl from creeping over surrounding farms. Over at Mexican Store Dos Amigos on Main Street, locals pop in for authentic cuts of meat and Mexican groceries, proving that beneath the misty winter stillness, this valley community retains a rich and varied everyday texture.

### **EP2**

February in Duvall calls for seeking shelter from persistent gray rain inside places that have stood for over a century. The historic building housing Valley House Brewing Co started out back in 1892 when Bob Crossman established it as a saloon, hotel, and stagecoach stop for weary valley travelers. Just down the road, the building that holds Duvall Flower & Gifts was constructed at Taylor's Landing in 1905, only to be loaded onto rails and physically hauled into town over six tedious weeks in 1909. Those layers of history give our modern streetscape a rooted quality you cannot fake.

For a clean two hour winter afternoon, head up to the Dougherty Farmstead on Cherry Valley Road to explore the grounds of the 1888 pioneer home and heritage orchard. After soaking in the valley lore, connect directly to the adjacent Coe-Clemons Creek trail. Walking this shaded path under tall cottonwoods offers a serene, low-sensory environment where the sound of rushing creek water completely drowns out the distant hum of vehicle traffic on the highway.

Daily routines during deep winter require knowing how to navigate small-town logistics without losing your patience. While downtown is easy to traverse on foot along flat sidewalks, driving remains essential if you are headed to outer farmsteads or hillside homes. When Main Street gridlock strikes during late afternoon, parallel routes on 1st Ave NE provide a clean bypass, and you can almost always find empty parking spots behind the shopfronts there.

On heavy downpour days, locals duck into cozy nooks like Cup of Sunshine Espresso or head over to the Duvall Library, which serves as a warm, inclusive community hub hosting public programs for all ages. Places like Crooked Label Brewing on Village Way offer friendly indoor gathering spots to escape the chill. Even in the dead of winter, the view across agricultural fields toward Mount Si from Taylor Landing Overlook reminds you why people have stayed in this valley for generations.

Cutting across West Snoqualmie River Rd NE down on the valley floor lets you sidestep the worst bottlenecks, while the public parking lot near Depot Park remains the most reliable spot to leave your car while doing daily errands around downtown.

Early spring is also prime time for dog walkers and runners to reclaim the Snoqualmie Valley Trail during crisp morning hours. The town stays grounded in its small-town ethos, where local food trucks like Tacos El Rey line up alongside historic storefronts. Taking a quick breather on a public bench at Taylor Landing Overlook lets you watch the valley wake up, proving that spring here arrives not with a roar, but with a steady, quiet renewal.

### **EP5**

May introduces long twilights and a quirky reminder of Duvall's counterculture roots. Back on April 28, 1968, nearly 3,000 music fans descended on a local farm to witness an upright piano dropped from a helicopter while Country Joe and the Fish performed live. That legendary event sparked the famous Sky River Rock Festival and proved this sleepy farming outpost always possessed an irreverent, independent edge beneath its quiet exterior.

Spend a golden May evening on a simple two hour stroll starting at Good Brewing on Main Street. Grab a cold beverage and amble past the low-rise historic shopfronts down to Depot Park. Join the dog walkers on the Snoqualmie Valley Trail and loop down toward McCormick Park, where open grassy meadows sit right along the edge of the rushing Snoqualmie River.

Moving around town in late spring requires a bit of local awareness as tourist traffic increases on regional routes. While central Duvall features continuous flat sidewalks and strong walkability along Main Street, heading anywhere up the surrounding hills still demands a car. Bypassing state route congestion by using West Snoqualmie River Rd NE allows you to move smoothly across the valley floor without getting stuck behind slow freight trucks.

May evenings invite long, zero-dollar hangs at the Taylor Landing Overlook, where you can watch evening light fade over the agricultural fields. The town's inclusive atmosphere shines bright at local gathering places, including the Duvall Library and casual eateries like Taco Queto. As the grass grows thick in the meadows, the entire valley feels primed for the coming summer season.

### **EP6**

June brings peak solstice energy, anchored by the annual Duvall Days Festival that transforms the center of town into a giant celebration. Main Street closes down for the Grand Parade, live music stages at Depot Park, and street markets that highlight our local heritage. The town's strict small-town design rules ensure that even during its biggest party of the year, the setting remains rooted in authentic early 1900s architecture rather than generic commercial clutter.

To experience the festival at its best, plan a two hour outing starting with a walk down Main Street during the vendor market. Pick up fresh bites from local spots like From V With Love or Mexican Store Dos Amigos, then wander down through Depot Park to catch live bands on the outdoor stage. Follow the crowd down to McCormick Park in the late afternoon to secure a spot on the lawn for evening fireworks over the river.

During festival weekend, vehicle navigation requires serious strategy due to Main Street closures. Drive-through commuters should avoid SR 203 altogether and use West Snoqualmie River Rd NE on the far side of the river. Public parking fills up early, so arriving well before midday to grab a spot in the gravel lots along 1st Ave NE is essential if you want to avoid driving in circles.

When the festival quiet down, the long June daylight keeps people outside until well past 9:00 PM. The Snoqualmie River, long honored by the Snoqualmie Tribe as a vital lifeline, becomes the focal point for evening gatherings. Whether sitting on public benches near Depot Park or watching the sunset from Taylor Landing Overlook, June delivers pure Pacific Northwest magic.
