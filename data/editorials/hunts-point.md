### **EP1**

Back in 1890, newspaper publisher Leigh S. J. Hunt bought this entire undeveloped peninsula for a singular purpose, which was to cut down the towering evergreens that blocked his view across Lake Washington. That impulse to control the landscape still echoes through the quiet streets today. When the town incorporated in 1955 to block annexation by Bellevue, residents doubled down on privacy by enforcing strict single-family zoning with zero commercial businesses. Today, mist rises off Cozy Cove in the post-holiday reset, leaving the town feeling like a private, fog-draped secret.

A quiet winter morning starts with a tranquil walk from Town Hall at 3000 Hunts Point Road down the shaded residential roadway past waterfront estates. Head to North of Main on 112th Ave NE for a hot espresso, or drop into Moms Table on NE 11th St for a warm drink to cut through the January chill.

Living here means accepting that you cannot walk to pick up a carton of milk. With no grocery stores or corner shops inside town boundaries, residents rely entirely on trips down to Bellevue Square or the Bellevue Way QFC for daily errands. Visitor parking is virtually non-existent along these narrow lanes, so anyone coming to visit must utilize the small civic lot at Town Hall. If you are commuting out during peak morning hours, avoid the bottleneck where Hunts Point Road NE meets 84th Ave NE by taking Points Drive NE eastbound toward Yarrow Point as your backdoor relief route into Kirkland.

Long before tech founders built waterfront estates along these banks, the ancestral waters belonged to the Duwamish, Snoqualmie, and Coast Salish peoples, who navigated the shoreline by canoe for seasonal fishing and harvesting. In mid-January, that deep, quiet connection to the lake is palpable. The town remains a sensory sanctuary with minimal traffic noise and heavy canopy shade. A crisp walk under the dripping Douglas firs offers a peaceful reset before diving back into the suburban routine.
