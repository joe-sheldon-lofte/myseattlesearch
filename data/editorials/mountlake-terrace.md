### **EP1**

Developer Albert LaPierre mapped out Mountlake Terrace in 1954 by clearing poultry farms and cut-over logging woods, laying down simple cinder-block homes for returning GIs. They called them cinderblock palaces back then, built on practical design and sweeping views of Lake Ballinger and Mount Rainier. That workaday foundation still anchors the town's identity today, even as high-rise developments grow around the light rail tracks. In the deep quiet of January, you can still feel that mid-century working-class spirit under the low fog.

When the post-holiday mist sits heavy over the neighborhood, start your morning down on the south side of town. Grab a warm hand-crafted drink at Skog Haus Coffee on Van Ry Blvd before heading toward Ballinger Park. Walk the wooden boardwalk circuit through the dormant wetlands out to the public fishing pier, where the distant Olympic Mountain silhouettes hide behind winter clouds. It is a peaceful two-hour routine that costs nothing and resets your head for the year ahead.

Getting around during these dark winter mornings requires a little tactical planning. When the southbound I-5 ramps at 220th St SW back up into a solid wall of brake lights, seasoned drivers dodge the gridlock by taking 228th St SW east or cutting down 66th Ave W to 244th St SW to hit Highway 99. You are not walking to run quick errands along the fast outer arterials, so knowing these local arterial bypasses saves real time when the weather is miserable.

January keeps the town's focus turned inward toward cozy indoor neighborhood spots. Long before high-density zoning transformed 236th St SW into a transit hub, this community relied on simple, unpretentious third places. You see it as rain drips off the storefront eaves along 56th Ave W, where long-time locals and newcomers share space, leaving the frantic pace of Seattle behind for a slower rhythm.
