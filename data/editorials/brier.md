### **EP1**

Post-holiday reset in Brier brings crisp air and low fog drifting over the old timberland ground. In 1965, residents incorporated on February 11 specifically to block annexation by Lynnwood and Mountlake Terrace, choosing to keep large lots, dark skies, and no streetlights. That defensive civic move preserved a community where dense woods still envelop quiet cul-de-sacs.

Grab a custom espresso at Jason's Java or step into Brier Grocery on Brier Road, then set off on the Brier Road and Bobcat Park loop. Walk south past historic single-family homesteads before turning east onto 236th St SW toward Bobcat Park. Resting on a bench under the dripping Douglas fir canopy lets you soak in the neighborhood stillness.

During morning rush hours (7:00 AM to 8:45 AM), the intersection at 228th St SW and Brier Road backs up steadily toward Bothell-Everett Highway. Smart commuters cut west along 236th St SW toward 56th Ave W to reach I-5 or the Mountlake Terrace Transit Center. Because there are no full-scale supermarkets within town limits, running out of milk means a quick stop at Brier Grocery or a short drive to the Mountlake Terrace QFC.

On wet winter mornings when steady rain taps against the evergreen canopy, the Brier Library inside City Hall offers sanctuary with floor-to-ceiling forest views. Long before suburban borders were drawn, Coast Salish ancestors traveled the Swamp Creek and Willow Creek corridors, lands known in Lushootseed as dxʷɬ(ə)q̓ab. Today, that deep connection to nature remains alive in Brier's quiet woodland corners.

### **EP2**

February marks Brier's incorporation anniversary, recalling the 1965 vote when neighbors banded together to preserve their woodsy lifestyle. Named after the dense, prickly wild brier bushes cleared by the Puget Mill Company, this two-square-mile enclave still confines all commercial activity to a single block at Brier Road and 236th St SW.

Try the Brierwood Park and Creek Trail circuit starting at 2655 Elm Drive. Walk the paved loop trail around the open playfield, follow the wooded greenbelt path along Locust Creek, and loop back along quiet equestrian shoulders where residents ride horses instead of fighting traffic.

Operating without gas stations or drive-thrus inside city limits requires planning daily errands in advance. Commuters heading west avoid morning congestion at 228th St SW by slipping down 236th St SW. If you need artisanal pastries or seasonal baked goods, Moon Rabbit Pastry along the Brier Road corridor provides a sweet morning treat without leaving town.

As cold rain falls across dark-sky residential streets, head to Vita Momenti Italian Kitchen on Brier Road for wood-fired pizza, pasta, or gyros. You can also cross the border to Hemlock State Brewing Co on 56th Ave W for a pint of craft beer with local regulars, enjoying the warm community atmosphere on a gray evening.

### **EP3**

Early spring in Brier arrives with sudden sun squalls breaking through gray clouds over the Snohomish County foothills. Finnish immigrants like the Salty family arrived here in 1883 to carve homesteads out of logged timberland, eventually donating land in 1896 for the town's first one-room schoolhouse. That early commitment to neighborhood community remains central to Brier's character.

Grab a warm drink at Jason's Java and wander over to Bobcat Park on 236th St SW. Sitting on a shady cedar perch while early spring buds open across the tree canopy gives you a real feel for the town's relaxed daily pace.

Living without continuous sidewalks means pedestrians share wide, paved road shoulders with cyclists, dog walkers, and horseback riders. Morning traffic around 228th St SW remains heavy, so bypassing the backup via 236th St SW toward 56th Ave W stays the smartest move for commuters.

When clear afternoon skies break through, head up to the elevated ridge on 228th St SW near 28th Ave W for sweeping, filtered views across the tree canopy toward Mount Rainier and the Cascade Mountains. On rainy days, retreat inside the Brier Library to read books against a dramatic backdrop of towering evergreens.
