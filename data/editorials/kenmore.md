### **EP1**

When Canadian lumberman John McMaster set down roots on January 1, 1901, and named this spot after his Scottish hometown, he was focused on cedar shingles rather than winter aesthetics. Back in the 1920s and 30s, people roared down the old Red Brick Road along Bothell Way to frequent dozens of rough around the edges roadhouses. Today, that old timber frontier energy gives way to a quiet post-holiday lull, where low fog hangs thick across northern Lake Washington and the pace slows down.

You can lean into the slow start with a simple two-hour morning ritual. Grab a hot espresso at Beans Coffee on Juanita Drive NE, then walk down to the Log Boom Park promenade. The six hundred foot public pier stretches right out into the gray mist, offering front row seats as Kenmore Air seaplanes (a fixture founded by Bob Munro back in 1946) lift off and touch down on the glassy water.

Navigating town during these damp winter stretches means accepting local traffic realities. Evening rush hour on Bothell Way (SR 522) tends to back up near 68th Ave NE, so local drivers often cut through NE 181st St or head up Juanita Drive NE to bypass the main line. Parking at Log Boom Park has a strict four-hour limit, but if the main lot is full, free street overflow is available along NE 175th St between 61st and 68th Ave NE. Downtown street parking around NE 181st St drops to a two-hour cap during daytime hours.

On the wettest mornings, the best move is escaping to The Hangar at Town Green to sit by the large double-sided fireplace. You can also head over to ƛ̕ax̌ʷadis Park to walk the elevated wooden paths. The name, pronounced Tl' awh-ah-dees, comes from the ancestral Lushootseed language and means a place where something is grown or sprouts, reminding you that quiet winter roots are always working beneath the surface.

### **EP2**

During the Great Depression, this stretch of shoreline hosted Voucherville, a WPA settlement where local workers were paid in vouchers to keep food on the table. Decades later in the Cold War, the town housed U.S. Army Nike Hercules missile batteries until 1974. That blue-collar resilience runs deep in the local soil, showing up in how neighbors quietly gather in warm corners when February skies remain stubbornly overcast.

For a thorough February reset, head over to Saint Edward State Park. You can take the forested loop trail down through massive Douglas firs all the way to the Lake Washington shoreline, listening to rain drop through the high canopy. After climbing back up to the main lawn, reward yourself with a craft drink at Father Mulligan's Heritage Bar inside The Lodge at St. Edward State Park, which sits in the restored 1931 seminary building.

Daily logistics around here require a car for most household runs once you head away from the core. While the block surrounding 68th Ave NE and NE 181st St allows you to walk between civic spaces and the QFC, living higher up Juanita Drive means driving is non-negotiable. If you are commuting home during peak evening hours, remember that Bothell Way westbound usually moves better than eastbound, where the 68th Ave NE intersection creates a predictable bottleneck.

Mid-winter chill calls for simple comfort food inside cozy local haunts. You might pull up a chair for tabletop games at Around the Table Game Pub or grab a table at Taqueria Tonita on NE Bothell Way for a hot plate of tacos. The town keeps its head down through the dark days, relying on indoor fireplaces and sturdy neighborhood spots to carry everyone through to spring.

### **EP6**

When Bob Munro co-founded Kenmore Air in 1946, he helped establish what would become one of the world's largest commercial seaplane bases. The roar of those classic floatplanes taking off over northern Lake Washington remains the definitive soundtrack of a local June, echoing off the surrounding hills as summer officially begins.

Spend a June evening at the Wednesday Kenmore Farmers Market right next to The Hangar at Town Green. Grab a baked treat from Backyard Bakery, browse local vendors, and then stroll down to the waterfront to watch seaplanes land smoothly on the lake under late solstice light.

Parking enforcement stays active near civic plazas during summer market season. Downtown street parking on NE 181st St maintains a strict two-hour limit between 8:00 AM and 6:00 PM, while Log Boom Park enforces its four-hour rule. If you plan to hang out for an extended afternoon, park along NE 175th St between 61st and 68th Ave NE for worry-free street parking.

June brings the annual Pride Flag raising ceremony at Civic Town Green, with local storefronts along NE 181st St displaying welcoming signage. Between community celebrations and nearly sixteen hours of daylight, the neighborhood feels interconnected and full of movement.
