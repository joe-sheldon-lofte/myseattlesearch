### **EP1**

January drops a heavy lid of low valley fog across the Snoqualmie Prairie, where the Snoqualmie Tribe historically managed the open grasslands through controlled burns to cultivate camas bulbs and berries. Mount Si disappears behind thick gray vapors, creating a quiet post-holiday reset across the valley floor. The cold air carries a clean winter stillness that makes you slow your pace.

An ideal morning starts by grabbing a hot espresso at Huxdotter Coffee on Park Street before heading over to Tollgate Farm Park. Walking the one-mile outer gravel trail loop gives you a front-row seat to resident Roosevelt elk grazing in the chilly mist, all framed against the hidden bulk of the mountain. It is an unhurried outing that lets the crisp weather clear your head.

Daily errands require a little strategy because retail is distinctly split in this town. You find small boutiques along Bendigo Boulevard and Railroad Avenue, while major big-box shopping centers sit near the Interstate 90 interchange on Mount Si Boulevard. You will definitely need your car keys for a full grocery run, but free two-hour street parking along Railroad Avenue makes downtown stops quick and easy.

Local history anchors the landscape near the North Bend Train Depot at William Henry Taylor Park, where timber trains once hauled giant logs from deep in the Cascades. Settled back in 1858 by Jeremiah Borst, the area retains its working-class roots. Ending your walk along the South Fork Snoqualmie River gravel bank brings a deep sense of foothill calm.

### **EP2**

February brings a distinct energy as fans gather around February 24th to celebrate Twin Peaks Day in the very town David Lynch picked for his cult series. Twede's Cafe on North Bend Way, famously known as the Double R Diner, survived a destructive arson fire in 2000 and was meticulously rebuilt. Its restored neon sign still draws visitors looking for cherry pie and a fine cup of coffee.

Ducking out of the winter drizzle for a craft pint at Volition Brewing on North Bend Way is an easy afternoon decision. Afterward, take a leisurely stroll along the adjacent Snoqualmie Valley Trail, crossing through William Henry Taylor Park to reach the river's edge. The cool air and rushing water provide a classic Pacific Northwest backdrop.

Navigating winter travel here comes with unique local wisdom. When sudden winter storms close Snoqualmie Pass and back up Interstate 90 westbound onto East North Bend Way, smart drivers avoid highway gridlock by using State Route 202 down to Fall City or taking Snoqualmie Valley Road toward Carnation. Knowing these back routes saves hours of frustration.

Beyond the television fame, Mount Si holds profound spiritual significance in Snoqualmie Tribe creation lore as a sacred high place. Long before William Taylor platted the township in 1889 under the name Snoqualmie Prairie Acres, this valley was defined by its dramatic rock faces. Modern city ordinances enforce strict height limits and dark-sky rules to keep those sweeping views untouched.

### **EP3**

March delivers unpredictable spring teases, swapping sun squalls for sudden icy rain showers within minutes. The sharp northward curve of the South Fork Snoqualmie River, which gave the town its permanent name, carries rushing mountain snowmelt through the valley. The entire landscape feels like it is stirring awake after a long sleep.

A great two-hour outing begins with a fresh brew at Arête on East North Bend Way, followed by a trip to Tanner Landing Park. Watching white-water rapids churn along the river from the park overlook gives you a vivid look at nature in motion.

Trailheads begin filling up as fair weather returns, making parking a prime consideration. The Little Si Trailhead lot fills before 7:30 AM on sunny weekends, so turning toward the overflow lot off Mount Si Road or Tanner Landing Park is necessary, especially since residential street parking along Mount Si Road is heavily ticketed and towed.

Timber and shingle mills powered by the North Bend Lumber Company once dominated these foothills after incorporation in 1909. Today, that industrial history lives on through converted landmarks like the North Bend Train Depot, now operated as a heritage boarding site by the Northwest Railway Museum.

### **EP4**

April brings a genuine spring awakening as daylight stretches past 7:30 PM and green shoots emerge across the valley floor. The massive vertical rock face of Mount Si emerges from winter clouds, dominating the horizon and inviting locals back outdoors.

Pick up a pastry at Chickadee Bakeshop on Railroad Avenue, then take a stroll over to Meadowbrook Farm. Walking the prairie paths gives you miles of open grassland trails jointly managed with Snoqualmie, offering unobstructed mountain views under warm afternoon light.

Getting around town highlights the functional divide between quick errands and major shopping. Boutique shops along Railroad Avenue and Park Street are easily walkable once you park in the free lot behind the train depot, but picking up hardware or bulk groceries means driving over to Mount Si Boulevard.

The town has always drawn creative minds, from science fiction writer Eric Nylund to legendary Seattle radio host Bob Rivers. The landscape itself provides endless inspiration, grounded in ancestral Snoqualmie Tribe homelands that have sustained life here for thousands of years.

### **EP5**

May introduces long twilights and pre-summer excitement. The evening air warms up, creating perfect conditions for one of the best free rituals in the region, watching resident Roosevelt elk emerge from the forest line at dusk to graze in the open pasture at Tollgate Prairie.

Grab a morning coffee at Freestone Collective on Railroad Avenue, a welcoming space known for its community vibe. From there, take a short drive to Gardiner Weeks Memorial Park for a tranquil, densely shaded riverfront walk that offers a quiet sensory retreat away from main arterial noise.

Summer hiker traffic begins building along highway corridors. To bypass weekend bottlenecks at Interstate 90 Exit 32, take Exit 31 onto Bendigo Boulevard, turn east on West North Bend Way, and use quiet side streets to reach Mount Si Road.

Local pride runs deep in North Bend, raised on foothill grit and community sports that produced talent like Seattle Reign FC soccer player Nikki Stanton. The sheer cliff face of Mount Si, carved by Ice Age glaciers, towers nearly 4,000 feet above, serving as a constant geographic compass.

### **EP6**

June arrives with solstice energy and lingering evening light that seems to last forever. Bicycles fill the Snoqualmie Valley Trail, and outdoor spaces along West North Bend Way come alive with residents enjoying the mild early summer climate.

Order authentic Mexican street tacos at Rio Bravo on East North Bend Way, then head over to Tanner Trail for a peaceful one-mile walk along a shaded gravel corridor removed from traffic noise.

Downtown parking remains accessible if you know where to look. While street spots along Bendigo Boulevard can fill during lunch hours, free unlimited parking along Park Street and the public lot behind the train depot make midday errands stress-free.

The North Bend Train Depot, built in 1889 by the Seattle, Lake Shore and Eastern Railway, stands as a monument to the valley's railroad logging era. Today, the surrounding lawns of William Henry Taylor Park offer a peaceful place to sit and watch the sunset cast long shadows across the valley.

### **EP7**

July brings peak summer heat, blue skies, and cool saltwater breezes drifting off Puget Sound into the river valley. Mid-month energy peaks with the Downtown North Bend Block Party, while Si View Park hosts free outdoor live music every Thursday evening during its summer concert series.

Stop by North Bend Fishnchips for a summer lunch, then head down to Tanner Landing Park to wade in the shallow river gravel bars and skip stones along the South Fork Snoqualmie River.

High summer crowds hit area attractions hard. To dodge heavy traffic on Cedar Falls Road when visiting Rattlesnake Lake and Cedar Falls Recreation Area, aim to arrive before 8:00 AM or after 5:00 PM on sunny days.

Indigenous history is present everywhere in the valley. The Snoqualmie Tribe managed these prairies for generations, and Mount Si remains a sacred landmark in regional oral history, maintaining a spiritual presence above the summer festivities.

### **EP8**

August arrives with golden hour light and late summer haze hanging over the Cascades. The annual Festival at Mount Si brings community parades, local vendors, and evening fireworks that light up the night sky against the looming silhouette of the mountains.

Treat yourself to sweet treats at Carousel Candy on Railroad Avenue, then stroll over to Tollgate Farm Park to sit on a view bench directly facing the vertical rock face of Mount Si's Haystack peak.

Parking logistics near popular hiking spots remain tight all month. Avoiding the main trail lots after mid-morning is essential, making lower valley parks like Tanner Landing Park or Meadowbrook Farm great alternatives for a peaceful afternoon.

Incorporated as a city in 1909, North Bend transitioned from a timber mill hub into an outdoor destination while maintaining strict dark-sky lighting guidelines. As night falls, the stars come out over dark valley skies uninterrupted by urban glare.

### **EP9**

September brings back-to-school crispness and low, warm autumn light. The high-summer crowds fade away, leaving quiet trails, clear air, and sharp autumn sunshine illuminating the Cascade foothill slopes.

Pick up a fresh brew at North Bend Coffeeroasters downtown, then take a leisurely walk along the Snoqualmie Valley Trail crossing William Henry Taylor Park down to the river bank.

Daily life settles back into a steady rhythm. Running errands across the dual retail hubs is smooth, with boutique stops along Railroad Avenue easily paired with quick trips to the big-box centers on Mount Si Boulevard.

The town was platted in 1889 as Snoqualmie Prairie Acres before adopting the name North Bend after the river's dramatic curve. That historical identity remains strong, blending small-town simplicity with immediate access to wild mountain landscapes.

### **EP10**

October coats the foothill forests in rich fall foliage, filling the air with the smell of damp earth and woodsmoke. Early dusk settles over the mountains, making indoor spots and hot comfort food especially inviting.

Enjoy a steaming bowl of traditional Vietnamese pho at Unphogettable on Mount Si Boulevard, then head over to Meadowbrook Farm for a peaceful autumn walk across the fog-dusted prairie paths.

Autumn rain turns dirt paths muddy, so opting for flat, paved routes like the accessible walkways at Tollgate Farm Park keeps your boots dry while still offering sweeping views of Mount Si's cloud-draped cliffs.

Historic buildings throughout town remind residents of the past, including original structures like Twede's Cafe on North Bend Way, which maintains its vintage diner counter and neon glow through rainy October evenings.

### **EP11**

November brings heavy Pacific Northwest winter rains, storm-watching weather, and early dark afternoons around 4:30 PM. The sound of rain on roofs creates a cozy, inward-looking atmosphere across the neighborhood.

Stop by Taqueria Entrada & Salida for authentic tacos or warm up inside Smokey Joe's or Mt. Si Tavern on North Bend Way, where local trade stories flow over cold drinks and hot pub food.

Winter weather often causes mountain pass closures. When heavy snow shuts down Snoqualmie Pass and backs up Interstate 90 traffic onto local streets, taking Snoqualmie Valley Road toward Carnation offers a reliable escape route.

The valley's ancestral roots go deep into Coast Salish history, where the Snoqualmie Tribe lived in harmony with seasonal rhythms. Today, that quiet endurance lives on as the community hunkers down for another foothill winter.

### **EP12**

December wraps downtown North Bend in festive holiday lights along Railroad Avenue, while fresh snowfall caps the summit of Mount Si. Winter cocoa runs, crisp night air, and community warmth define the final month of the year.

Pick up fresh baked goods at Snoqualmie Pie on Railroad Avenue, then wander through William Henry Taylor Park to view the historic railway cars illuminated under holiday lights.

Holiday shopping is straightforward with free street parking along Railroad Avenue and Park Street, letting you browse local boutiques for gifts before running to Mount Si Boulevard for household supplies.

First settled by Jeremiah Borst in 1858, North Bend has grown from a remote prairie homestead into a resilient foothill town. Through every season, Mount Si stands guard over the valley, welcoming another year in the Pacific Northwest.
