### **EP1**

Luke McRedmond and Warren Perrigo homesteaded this river valley in 1871, when the waterway was so packed with returning fish that locals called the settlement Salmonberg. It took the arrival of the Seattle Lake Shore and Eastern Railway in 1888 to transform logging camps into a proper municipality, eventually incorporating in 1912 just to tax local saloons and build a real water network. Long before loggers or tech campuses claimed the banks, Snoqualmie and Duwamish ancestors gathered along Bear Creek, known in Lushootseed as x̌obal, harvesting resources from soil that hosted human activity for over twelve thousand years.

January brings a slow, restorative quiet to the valley that you can best experience on foot starting around Cleveland Street. Grab a hot drink at Redmond Coffeeroasters Spotlight downtown, then head south along the paved Sammamish River Trail past Dudley Carter Park, where the hand-carved Haida House timber structure stands in winter mist. Cross the pedestrian bridge into Marymoor Park and stroll around the quiet grounds of the historic Clise Mansion, watching the occasional model airplane cut through the low ceiling at the remote-controlled airfield.

Getting around in the dead of winter means respecting the divide between the flat valley floor and the surrounding hills. Downtown offers connected, flat sidewalk paths and two-hour free street parking, but if you are running errands up on Education Hill or English Hill, you are bound to your vehicle along Redmond Way or NE 104th Street. If you plan to jump on light rail at the Marymoor Village Light Rail Station or Overlake Technology Station, use the official park-and-ride lots instead of trying to sneak into private retail garages that actively tow.

January days are dominated by low-hanging fog hovering over Lake Sammamish, giving zero-dollar vantage points like Idylwood Beach Park a moody, contemplative air. The public lawn seating sits quiet, offering peaceful water views looking clear across toward Bellevue without the summer crowds. If the drizzle turns heavy, retreat to low-stimulus sanctuaries like the Redmond Regional Library or the covered arcades at Redmond Town Center to wait out the rain.
