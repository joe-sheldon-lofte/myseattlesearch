### **EP1**

January brings a quiet, reflective reset across the Stillaguamish River valley. This land carries deep history, from the ancestral homelands of the Stillaguamish Tribe of Indians who have stewarded these river mouths for millennia to the late 19th-century Scandinavian settlers who logged the timber and gave the area its historic nickname, Snoose Hill. When the post-holiday fog settles low over the delta, the town returns to a slow, unhurried rhythm that belongs entirely to the locals.

A cold morning is best started with a warm pour over at Black Swan Coffee Roasting or a fresh pastry from Cookie Mill & Eatery. From there, head west toward the Stillaguamish River Dike Overlook for a quiet two-hour walk along the earthen trail. High tide at Irvine Slough offers front-row seats to thousands of wintering snow geese and tundra swans resting on the tidal flats against a backdrop of muted gray skies.

Living here during the winter means navigating a town split into distinct hubs. While West Stanwood along 271st St NW holds walkable independent storefronts, high-volume grocery runs send you out toward the SR-532 corridor near 72nd and 88th Ave NW. Your car is essential for daily errands, but free public parking in the municipal lots behind 271st St NW makes popping into local shops completely painless.

By late afternoon, the damp salt air rolls off Port Susan and settles into the valley floor. Twilight arrives early, pushing everyone toward cozy indoor window seats with a hot drink. It is a season of quiet sanctuary, where the raw beauty of the tideflats meets the steady warmth of a tight-knit river town.

### **EP2**

February arrives with a distinct spark of energy courtesy of Glass Quest, the annual hand-blown glass treasure hunt that sends locals scanning park trails and storefronts. The quest highlights the unique layout of Stanwood, born from the historic Twin City rivalry. When the Great Northern Railway bypassed the original town center in 1891 to avoid seasonal river floods, East Stanwood sprang up a mile away. The two towns maintained separate schools, banks, and governments for decades until consolidating in 1960.

Between glass clues, spend a cozy couple of hours grabbing lunch at SAAL Brewing Company on 270th St NW for house-brewed lagers and fresh beer-battered wild Alaskan cod fish and chips. Afterwards, take a short stroll past the D.O. Pearson House, the 1890 Victorian residence built by the town postmaster that now anchors the local historical society complex.

When winter rain squalls pick up, smart locals move the day indoors to Brewcade Pinball & Taphouse for retro arcade games and local drafts. Driving through town stays simple enough, though avoiding main road slowdowns means taking 268th St NW as a parallel route around the rail corridor. Free public parking behind West Stanwood and at the Stanwood Amtrak Depot ensures you never have to search long for a spot.

The atmosphere this month balances crisp winter air with the playful thrill of the glass hunt. Gray skies dominate the horizon, but inside local haunts, the mood is bright, friendly, and deeply connected to neighborhood history.

### **EP3**

March delivers the first teases of Pacific Northwest spring, where sudden sun breaks cut through passing rain squalls. This landscape has long inspired thinkers and artists, including theologian Eugene H. Peterson, who wrote The Message Bible while drawing quiet inspiration from the Stillaguamish River valley, and Forest Service ranger Nels Bruseth, who painted the backcountry wilderness. That same quiet, creative spirit still runs through the valley as the green returns.

Take advantage of a dry afternoon by grabbing street tacos from Taqueria Mi Cocina, then head over to Church Creek Park. A quiet walk through the wooded trails under a thick Douglas fir canopy leads right into the public 18-hole disc golf course. Loop back through adjacent Heritage Park for a wide-open view of the valley hills.

Spring weather in the valley requires a bit of tactical planning. West Stanwood features wide, flat concrete sidewalks that make walking between local shops comfortable even in a drizzle, but river dike trailheads fill up quickly the moment the sun breaks through. For daily grocery runs, keeping to SR-532 works best outside of peak afternoon hours.

Evening skies in late March bring sharp, dramatic sunsets over the tideflats. The return of migratory songbirds adds a subtle background chorus to the river, signaling that the long dark of winter is officially breaking.

### **EP4**

April brings an unmistakable awakening across the agricultural flatlands, with daylight stretching comfortably past 7:30 PM. The shift is visible in West Stanwood along 271st St NW, where mid-century berry and pea processing plants like the old Twin City Foods complex have been repurposed into artisan production spaces and community hubs, bridging the town's farming heritage with its creative present.

Start an April morning with a quick stop at Cedarhome Espresso for a specialty drive-thru drink, then head down to the Old Stanwood Fire Hall to explore its transformed retail spaces. Spend the rest of the morning enjoying a quiet bench along the Stillaguamish River dike, watching the valley floor turn brilliant shades of spring green.

As farm work picks up, tractor traffic increases on rural back roads, reminding everyone to share the pavement. Splitting daily chores between the independent pharmacies on 271st St NW and the larger retail plazas near 88th Ave NW requires driving, but free parking throughout both historic downtown zones keeps things low stress.

Patio coffee drops and outdoor conversations become the daily norm. Cherry blossoms frame the low-rise historic streetscapes, while strict 35 to 45 foot commercial height limits ensure the surrounding views of the Cascades remain totally open.

### **EP5**

May brings long twilights and a surge of pre-summer energy across the Stillaguamish delta. The town carries a quiet pride in its notable homegrown figures, from NHL Stanley Cup champion T.J. Oshie to pioneering illustrator Fanny Cory. That unpretentious, hard-working local identity is on full display as neighbors emerge to enjoy the warming evening air.

Pick up a massive breakfast burrito or carne asada from Raliberto's Taco Shop, then head to Heritage Park for an unscripted afternoon outing. Walk the half-mile paved loop, let the dog run in the public dog park, and finish by sitting on the lawn as twilight settles over the sports pavilion.

As temperatures rise, Friday afternoon traffic heading toward Camano Island over the SR-532 bridge starts to back up between 3 PM and 7 PM. Locals know to use 268th St NW or 269th Pl NW as a parallel bypass to clear East Stanwood without getting trapped in the weekend beach queue.

Late May evenings are legendary here, with clear skies offering panoramic views of Mt. Baker from the river levees. The air smells of freshwater, saltwater, and cut grass, creating a pure Pacific Northwest atmosphere.

### **EP6**

June arrives with solstice energy, endless daylight, and the return of maritime gatherings. Long before Euro-American settlement, the Stillaguamish Tribe maintained permanent villages near the river mouth, maintaining a deep cultural connection to these waters that continues through modern environmental stewardship. June honors that aquatic bond as life shifts entirely outdoors.

Spend a Saturday morning browsing the Stanwood Farmers Market before heading to SAAL Brewing Company for a cold craft ale on 270th St NW. Follow it up with a slow two-hour walk through West Stanwood, crossing over the river dike to take in sweeping summer views across Port Susan bay.

With summer tourism in full swing, popular spots like English Boom Trail County Park draw larger crowds. To get a quiet stroll along the boardwalk, plan your visit before 9 AM or time it precisely with high tide for the best birdwatching without the foot traffic.

Twilight lingering past 9:00 PM gives June an expansive, unhurried feeling. Evening walks down 271st St NW feature warm breezes off the sound, live music floating out of local taprooms, and a relaxed small-town rhythm.

### **EP7**

July delivers peak Pacific Northwest summer, bringing 80-degree weather, saltwater breezes, and sparkling valley views. Internationally acclaimed photographer Francesca Woodman spent part of her early childhood in Stanwood, and it is easy to see how the stark, dramatic light of the tideflats captivated her eye during these dry summer months.

Treat yourself to hand-dipped ice cream or a fresh baked slice of pie at Journey's End Cafe, then make the short drive to English Boom Trail County Park. The free boardwalks and view benches provide an incredible front-row seat to Skagit Bay, Whidbey Island, and the distant Olympic Mountains during low tide.

Navigating Stanwood in July requires respect for the SR-532 corridor, which serves as the sole vehicular gateway connecting mainland Washington to Camano Island over the Camano Gateway Bridge. Midday weekend traffic can crawl, so timing errands for early mornings or using local back streets is essential for keeping your sanity.

Summer nights are pure magic, with cool marine air blowing in off the saltwater to flush out the heat of the day. Neighbors gather on porch steps, local beaches, and open park lawns to soak up every drop of sunshine.

### **EP8**

August brings golden hour hangs, late-season agricultural harvests, and the beloved Stanwood Camano Fair. The fair celebrates the town's agricultural roots, where mid-century berry and pea farms laid the groundwork for today's community culture. It is a time when local youth, farmers, and long-time residents gather to honor rural traditions.

Grab a plate of hand-battered Alaskan cod fish and chips from Farmer's Cafe, then spend an afternoon sipping cold beers at SAAL Brewing Company or playing retro pinball at Brewcade Pinball & Taphouse. Afterward, head to the Stillaguamish River Dike to watch the sunset turn the dry harvest fields deep gold.

Fair week brings extra traffic, but public parking remains stress-free if you know where to look. The municipal lots behind West Stanwood and the Stanwood Amtrak Depot offer plenty of free space within easy walking distance of downtown dining and shopping.

Late August carries a warm, hazy atmosphere as smoke and dust settle softly over the valley. Evenings remain warm enough for lingering outdoor drinks, with berry stands operating at full tilt along the highway corridors.

### **EP9**

September introduces a crisp back-to-school atmosphere, balanced by warm, sunny afternoons and clear autumn light. The town's name itself traces back to a romantic historic origin when founding postmaster Daniel O. Pearson renamed the settlement of Centerville to Stanwood in 1877 to honor his wife, Clara Stanwood Pearson, and satisfy federal postal demands.

Start a September morning with drip coffee and an oversized cookie from Cookie Mill & Eatery. Take a quiet two-hour walk along the English Boom Trail, where shoulder-season quiet returns to the boardwalks and sharp autumn light highlights the Cascade peaks across the valley.

Daily life slows back down to a comfortable local rhythm as summer beach traffic fades. Running errands between the independent hardware stores in West Stanwood and the larger retail outlets off 88th Ave NW becomes quick and effortless once again.

Mornings carry a sharp, invigorating chill, followed by warm, golden afternoons. The changing leaves along Church Creek Park mark the official arrival of autumn, bringing a calm, grounded mood to the neighborhood.

### **EP10**

October turns the Stillaguamish River valley into a tapestry of deep reds and golds. As autumn rain settles in, the history of the 1960 consolidation between East and West Stanwood feels visible in the dual commercial cores that still define the town's architecture, both protected by height limits that keep the valley sky wide open.

Warm up with a bowl of soup or noodle specialty at Chu Bistro, then head to Church Creek Park for a leafy autumn walk. The dense canopy of Douglas firs and deciduous trees provides a beautiful cover against light rain while you explore the trails or throw a casual round of disc golf.

Farming activity shifts to pumpkin patches and harvest stands, bringing brief slow-downs on rural connectors. When traffic builds on the main strip, 269th Pl NW offers a reliable workaround for residents heading across town for daily supplies.

Damp earth scents, early dusks, and rain-slicked asphalt characterize October evenings. It is the perfect time to pull on a wool sweater, grab a seat at a neighborhood table, and enjoy the cozy turn of the seasons.

### **EP11**

November brings the first real winter storms, dark afternoons by 4:30 PM, and the steady sound of rain on tin roofs. The Scandinavian pioneers who settled Snoose Hill built the first Norwegian Lutheran Church in the Pacific Northwest near the river in 1876, establishing a tradition of seeking sturdy sanctuary during these dark coastal winters.

Grab a fresh micro-roasted coffee from Black Swan Coffee Roasting, then head to the coast for a brief, dramatic storm-watching session at English Boom. When the wind picks up, retreat indoors to Brewcade Pinball & Taphouse to play classic arcade games and sample rotating local craft drafts.

Navigating dark, rainy November days requires staying close to home. Parking directly in front of or behind West Stanwood's 271st St NW storefronts allows you to dart between local shops without getting soaked, while free depot parking handles quick rail commutes.

The atmosphere is quiet, intimate, and comforting. Low clouds rest directly on the river bends, pushing everyone toward warm dining rooms, hot drinks, and steady neighborhood conversation.

### **EP12**

December wraps Stanwood in festive lights and community warmth across both historic downtown corridors. From 270th St NW in East Stanwood to 271st St NW in West Stanwood, holiday displays illuminate the historic Victorian streetscapes, echoing over a century of shared small-town tradition.

Enjoy a sit-down Mexican dinner with margaritas at Ixtapa Stanwood Family Mexican Restaurant or Amigo Family Mexican Restaurant & Bar. Afterward, take a cold, crisp night stroll past the Old Stanwood Fire Hall and local boutique shops to pick up unique holiday gifts.

Holiday traffic around the commercial centers on SR-532 can spike, but locals easily bypass the main bottlenecks by utilizing 268th St NW to navigate between local gift spots without stress. Ample free parking across municipal lots makes holiday errands simple.

December in Stanwood is defined by glowing store windows, cold starry nights, and a deep sense of community togetherness. It is a classic Pacific Northwest winter finale, grounded in history, warmth, and river valley pride.
