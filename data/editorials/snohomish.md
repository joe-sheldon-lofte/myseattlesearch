### **EP1**

Edson T. Cady and Emory C. Ferguson founded Cadyville back in 1859, long before the town lost its status as county seat to Everett after a heated 1897 vote. The original Coast Salish Sdokohwalb people knew these riverways intimately, trading near the winter village of sbadaʔɬ long before timber axes arrived. That quiet, stubborn survival spirit still permeates First Street during the cold post-holiday reset.

Grab a hot espresso from Snohomish Coffeeroasters Spotlight downtown and take a morning walk along First Street to admire the 19th-century brick facades. Head down the access ramp at Cady Park and join the Snohomish Riverfront Trail for a peaceful one-mile walk west toward the river bridge overlook, watching low fog cling to the riverbank while bald eagles hunt from bare cottonwood branches.

Do not expect to run your routine household errands on First Street, as those brick blocks are strictly reserved for antique shoppers and weekend wanderers. Locals do their grocery runs and hardware pickups up along the Bickford Avenue corridor or the Avenue D arterial. On icy January mornings, you can skip main street congestion by slipping down Avenue B to reach northern residential neighborhoods.

January brings a heavy, silver stillness to the valley floor. Low mist sits over the floodplain while rain taps gently on metal roofs along the riverfront. It is a slow, reflective month where the town catches its breath, offering quiet sanctuaries inside warm cafes and historical nooks before the spring rush returns.

### **EP2**

The Blackman brothers, Alanson, Elhanan, and Hyrcanus, transformed this timber settlement into the shingle capital of the world after Elhanan invented the tripper shingle machine. Their 1883 steam locomotive helped ship red cedar across the country, and you can still feel that industrial grit preserved inside local landmarks like the Blackman House Museum on Avenue B.

Escape the late-winter gloom with a two-hour indoor wander through the 5-story Star Center Antique Mall at 201 1st St, where over 175 independent dealers display vintage treasures. Afterward, cross over to Avenue A and grab a table for warm soup inside the Cabbage Patch Restaurant, an 1800s Victorian home featuring cozy parlor dining.

Finding a parking spot on First Street during weekend lunch hours can test anyone's patience. Bypass the search entirely by pulling into the free municipal lot behind the historic Carnegie Building at 105 Cedar Ave or finding street parking along Avenue C. If you are heading out of town during the evening commute, remember that the Avenue D Bridge and SR 9 interchange gridlock early, so cutting down Riverview Road via Airport Way saves valuable time.

February brings gray skies and cool drizzles, turning the Snohomish River into a dark ribbon winding through the valley. It is the perfect excuse to sink into cozy booth seating, listen to local lore about the famously haunted Oxford Saloon, and appreciate how the town's strict 26-block Historic Preservation District keeps corporate architecture completely at bay.

### **EP3**

Long before Earl Averill earned his nickname as The Earl of Snohomish and went on to the Baseball Hall of Fame, this valley was defined by the confluence of the Snohomish and Pilchuck Rivers. The local sports legacy, celebrated at Earl Averill Field near Snohomish High School, reflects a tight-knit community pride that carries straight into early spring.

Kick off a crisp March afternoon at the Centennial Trail Snohomish Trailhead off Pine Street. Walk north along the flat paved rail-trail corridor for a mile and a half, loop west onto Avenue B past historic homes, and pause for a quiet rest at Ferguson Park along the tree-lined shoreline of Blackmans Lake.

March weather in the Pacific Northwest is notoriously unpredictable, shifting from sun squalls to sudden downpours in minutes. If you get caught in a cloudburst near downtown, duck inside the Carnegie Building or pop into a nearby boutique like Annie's on First or Joy Works. For daily errands, stick to Bickford Avenue so you do not get stuck behind slow-moving tourists touring the Historic District.

Daylight begins stretching past 6:00 PM, casting sharp late-afternoon light across the agricultural valley. The air smells of damp earth and fresh buds as local plant lovers prepare for garden season. It is a time of quiet transition, where winter chills give way to brief bursts of golden sun over the surrounding farmlands.

### **EP5**

The Snohomish River valley has always been an agricultural engine, from early timber camps to historic family farms along Marsh Road and Riverview Road. The Tulalip Tribes maintain vital cultural and fishing ties to this watershed under the 1855 Treaty of Point Elliott, honoring centuries of Coast Salish stewardship over these fertile lands.

Visit the opening weeks of the Snohomish Farmers Market held every Thursday at 105 Cedar Ave outside the Carnegie Building. Browse regional farm displays and listen to live acoustic music before taking a short walk down to the Snohomish Riverfront Trail for an evening stroll as the sun dips low over the valley plain.

Thursday afternoon market crowds add noticeable friction to downtown traffic along Cedar Avenue and First Street. Save yourself time by parking in the municipal lot behind the Carnegie Building early or opting for street spots along Avenue C. ### **EP7**

Take in the peak summer energy during Kla Ha Ya Days by watching the annual parade along First Street before heading south to Harvey Field for the Hot Air Balloon Glow. Position yourself near the riverbank at low tide to watch hot air balloons drift over the valley while eagles circle overhead.

July festival weekend brings peak traffic gridlock, especially where SR 9 meets SR 2 and along the Avenue D Bridge. Avoid main street thoroughfares by using 13th Street to reach northern neighborhoods or taking Riverview Road for south-bound escapes. Remember that antique shops open at 10:00 AM, so early morning is your best bet for quiet browsing before tour buses arrive.

Peak summer delivers 80-degree afternoons, saltwater breezes drifting up the river, and golden hour hues over the plain. The town operates outdoors, from riverfront picnics at Kla Ha Ya Park to evening strolls along the dike. It is a celebratory, sun-soaked month defined by aviation displays, community parades, and warm river breezes.

Stories like these reflect how Snohomish holds tightly to its colorful, unvarnished history through every season.

When winter rains roll in, spend a cozy afternoon exploring multi-story antique centers along First Street or browsing rare books downtown.
