### **EP1**

January brings a quiet reset to the valley. Long before this river basin became a national destination for wine lovers, Ira and Susan Woodin homesteaded 160 acres here in 1871 along what was then known as Squak Slough. They cleared timber and built a life around the water, setting down roots in a valley that spent winters wrapped in low fog and silent river mist. That calm winter mood still returns every January, clearing out the weekend crowds and handing the town back to the people who actually live here.

When the morning fog hangs heavy over the water, head downtown for a quick hot coffee at Mercurys Coffee Co before taking a quiet walk. Start at Wilmot Gateway Park and follow the flat, paved Sammamish River Trail as it curves south along the quiet agricultural farmland. After a chilly mile along the water, warm up inside the Fireside Lounge at Willows Lodge or grab a table at Purple Cafe, where the massive stone fireplaces offer the ultimate winter refuge.

Getting daily errands done during these dark, damp weeks is blessedly straightforward because almost everything essential is compressed into a compact three-block strip along NE 175th Street. You can hit Haggen, Target, Home Depot, and the Post Office in a single quick loop without ever touching the highway. You do not come here expecting a high-density urban core where you walk to get groceries in the snow, so keeping your car warm and running your errands in one swift downtown pass is just part of the local winter routine.

The valley takes on a deeply peaceful rhythm this time of year, far removed from the summer rush. Long before settlers logged these banks, the Snoqualmie Tribe and Duwamish people navigated Squak Slough in cedar canoes, treating this fertile corridor as a essential seasonal highway. Standing on the Wilmot River Bridge on a clear January afternoon, looking out at the cold river framed by distant snowcapped peaks, you feel that deep historical connection to the land and water that still defines life in the valley.

### **EP2**

February in the valley is all about dodging gray drizzle and seeking out dark, cozy corners. Back in 1888, the arrival of the Seattle-Lake Shore and Eastern Railway transformed this quiet agricultural clearing into a roaring junction depot. The tracks brought saloons, general stores, and rugged hotels, turning a scattered logging outpost into a genuine commercial town. Today, that old railway corridor forms the backbone of modern transit, but the instinct to seek out good food and strong drinks on a rainy day remains unchanged.

Beat the afternoon gloom by picking up a box of fresh hazelnut mousse cakes or kouign-amann from SheChill Patisserie, a standout French-Asian artisan bakery right in town. Take your treats over to Woodin Creek Village, where curbless, ADA-accessible pedestrian walkways make for an easy, puddle-free stroll even when the skies are dropping steady Northwest rain. Afterward, slide into a booth for a warm afternoon cup of craft tea at Bobae Coffee & Tea, famous for its house-made boba and nitro teas.

If you are planning to visit the tasting rooms over in the Warehouse District off 144th Ave NE on a February weekend, timing is everything. Show up before 11:30 AM to beat the mid-day rush, because large groups and tour buses start packing into those compact industrial spaces by early afternoon. Knowing when to move and when to lay low is the key to enjoying the city without feeling trapped in an industrial park parking lot.

The local master planning strictly preserves the Sammamish River agricultural basin, directing modern residential growth into dense nodes like Woodin Creek Village while keeping building heights modest across the city. This deliberate design preserves a surprising amount of open space for winter wildlife watching. Take a quiet, low-stimulus walk down the shaded river trail at Woodin Creek Park, where you can spot bald eagles and Great Blue Herons hunting along the banks in the cool February light.

### **EP3**

March brings sudden sun squalls and the first subtle hints of spring across the valley. Look closely at the commercial buildings around town and you will spot layers of history hiding in plain sight. The Carol Edwards Center began as a humble one-room schoolhouse in 1892, grew into a sturdy brick school in 1909, received a major WPA expansion in 1935, and now serves as the community's primary civic hub. That continuous transformation mirrors how the city repeatedly invents itself while keeping its structural bones intact.

Take advantage of an early spring sunbreak with a two-hour outing along the river. Park your car at Wilmot Gateway Park and head south on the Sammamish River Trail, watching the valley trees start to show their first green buds. Walk a mile and a half down to NE 145th Street, then cross over to Pintxo by Valdemar for an afternoon of authentic Spanish tapas, Washington wines, and cured seafood delicacies before enjoying a brisk walk back along the water.

As sunshine returns, weekend gridlock inevitably flares up at the intersection of NE 145th Street and Highway 202 in the Hollywood District. Local drivers avoid this seasonal choke point by taking 132nd Ave NE through Downtown via NE 171st Street, or by cutting in from the south along 148th Ave NE from Redmond. Skipping the primary highway intersection saves you twenty minutes of idling behind out-of-town wine drivers.

March is also when the agricultural roots of the area start waking up. Over a century ago in 1912, Seattle lumber baron Frederick Stimson established Hollywood Farm right here, setting world milk production records with his Holstein cattle before the estate evolved into Chateau Ste. Michelle in 1976. Today, over 130 wineries and tasting rooms operate in town without growing a single commercial grape locally, acting as the primary tasting capital for Eastern Washington vineyards.

### **EP4**

April brings blooming cherry blossoms, daylight stretching past 7:30 PM, and a sudden surge of outdoor energy. Long before modern wine tasting rooms filled the area, the Snoqualmie Tribe lived along Squak Slough, utilizing the calm river waters as an essential canoe route between inland villages and Puget Sound. That deep relationship with the water is still felt every spring as locals head back down to the riverbanks to watch the seasonal shift unfold.

Celebrate the longer afternoons by heading to Woodin Creek Plaza for a fresh drink from Luv Market & Cafe or a cup of nitro tea from Bobae. Walk the paved, flat trails through Woodin Creek Park down to the river loop, taking in the fresh spring air and early tree blooms. Afterwards, head over to Plaza Santa Fe, a long-standing family-owned downtown fixture, for traditional Mexican platters and fresh margaritas on the patio.

Daily logistics in April require a quick mental adjustment as regional visitors return to the valley. While commercial essentials like grocery runs and hardware trips are efficiently packed along NE 175th Street, traveling between the residential neighborhoods and the southern tasting districts requires a short drive or a quick rideshare. Keeping your running errands confined to weekday mornings lets you avoid the growing weekend tourist traffic entirely.

The city balances its expanding multicultural food scene with historic preservation, creating a rich neighborhood atmosphere. You can grab French-Asian pastries at SheChill Patisserie, modern regional Mexican dishes at Cava Azul, or traditional street tacos at Lifted Taco all within a five-minute drive. As the afternoon sun warms the valley, local families gather at DeYoung Park, a quiet pocket park built on the historic site of a 1910s produce packing shed and feed mill.

### **EP5**

May delivers pre-summer energy, warm twilights, and perfect patio weather across the valley. The city's history is full of clever transformations, none more legendary than The Hollywood Tavern. Built back in 1947 as a rural roadside tavern and gas station, it has evolved into an iconic spot for craft cocktails and scratch-made food, all while preserving its original historic neon sign and classic outdoor fire pit.

Make the most of a mild May evening by starting at Wilmot Gateway Park for a walk along the river trail. Head south toward the Hollywood District, taking in the views of green fields and distant hills as the sun drops. Finish your loop at The Hollywood Tavern, securing a spot around the outdoor fire pit for a cold drink and a plate of tavern fare while the evening air cools down.

As warm weather arrives, driver frustration spikes along the main valley arteries on sunny weekend afternoons between 1 PM and 5 PM. To bypass the worst of the congestion around the southern wineries, use 148th Ave NE coming up from Redmond to access the Hollywood District from the south. It is the cleanest way to dodge the backup on Highway 202 without getting stuck in endless stop-and-go traffic.

The town's unique athletic culture also shines through its notable residents who grew up playing on local fields. Figures like NFL Pro Bowl long snapper Jon Dorenbos, UFC veteran Lisa Ellis, and UW volleyball head coach Leslie Gabriel all trace their roots back to these same high school grounds and local parks. That proud homegrown spirit is easy to spot on May evenings, when youth sports and dog walkers fill the open lawns at Wilmot Gateway Park.

### **EP6**

June brings solstice light that lingers near 9:00 PM, lush green valley foliage, and non-stop outdoor living. Early Woodinville was built on raw timber, powered by the Woodinville Shingle Mill, Machias Sawmill, and Saginaw Sawmill. Loggers used Squak Slough to float massive logs down to Lake Washington, laying the industrial groundwork for the town. Today, those ancient logging routes have become scenic greenbelts where residents spend their long summer evenings.

Kick off a warm June afternoon at Watts Brewing Company, a small independent brewery inspired by family beekeeping, and grab a pint on their sunny outdoor patio. Afterwards, head over to Gold Creek County Park for a peaceful walk along the elevated forest dirt loops. The park leads to the Gold Creek Overlook, a quiet public bench offering zero-dollar views looking out over the upper Sammamish Basin forest canopy.

When summer events start filling up the calendar, venue parking can turn into an expensive headache. Chateau Ste. Michelle charges hefty fees for concert parking and exiting the lot can easily take forty-five minutes after a show. The local secret is to park for free at Wilmot Gateway Park or Woodin Creek Park, then walk or ride a bike one mile down the flat, paved Sammamish River Trail directly to the venue gates.

The community spirit in June is completely unpretentious, reflected in creative locals like indie musician and producer Harrison Patrick Smith, known internationally as The Dare, who grew up right here in town. On warm weekends, residents skip the crowded tourist spots to gather at Bosk Brew Works in the Warehouse District for crisp lagers, enjoying a relaxed neighborhood atmosphere that feels a million miles away from corporate PR polish.

### **EP7**

July brings peak Northwest summer, with temperatures hovering in the warm eighties, cool saltwater breezes floating in from the Sound, and long afternoons by the river. The seasonal calendar officially kicks off around mid-July with the start of the Celebrate Woodinville summer concert series. Every Wednesday evening, locals bring lawn chairs and blankets down to Wilmot Gateway Park for free live music, local food trucks, and family lawn games under the sun.

For an unscripted summer afternoon, start at Woodin Creek Village and grab an iced drink from Luv Market & Cafe or a specialty treat from Seatown Sweets. Walk along the curbless pedestrian paths down to Wilmot Gateway Park and cross the Wilmot River Bridge. This pedestrian crossing offers a zero-dollar view of Mount Rainier framed directly over the river valley on clear July days, making it one of the best photo spots in town.

Summer weekend traffic around the valley reaches its absolute peak in July, especially when concert crowds mix with tasting room visitors. Driving across NE 145th Street between 1 PM and 5 PM requires patience, so smart locals plan their daily errands early in the morning along NE 175th Street. Getting your grocery shopping done at Haggen before 10 AM keeps your weekend completely stress-free.

The river corridor remains the beating heart of local summer life, offering a cool sanctuary when the afternoons heat up. The flat, completely paved grade of the Sammamish River Trail makes it fully accessible for strollers, wheelchairs, and mobility scooters, allowing everyone to enjoy the water. Whether you are watching kayakers paddle past or taking a shady break under mature trees, the river provides a refreshing escape from the summer heat.

### **EP8**

August brings late summer haze, long golden hours, and two of the city's most cherished annual traditions. Mid-August marks the arrival of the annual community parade and its legendary Basset Hound Walk along NE 175th Street. Hundreds of droopy-eared basset hounds lead the parade through downtown, drawing crowds of smiling locals for an unpretentious celebration that captures the town's fun, community-first identity.

Spend a warm August evening enjoying great food and open scenery. Start at Lifted Taco in the Hollywood District for glazed shrimp or wine-drunk pork belly street tacos. Afterwards, take a short drive over to Cottage Lake Park and walk out onto the wooden public boardwalk, where you can sit on the pier, watch the water lilies, and enjoy the golden evening light reflecting off the lake.

Navigating the town during peak summer concert season requires knowing how to maneuver around the big venues. When major shows roll into the Chateau Ste. Michelle estate grounds, traffic along Highway 202 comes to a crawl. Parking at Wilmot Gateway Park or downtown public lots and walking the shaded river trail lets you skip the post-concert gridlock entirely, getting you home while other drivers are still stuck in the venue parking lot.

The late summer heat makes cold drinks and shady patios absolute necessities. Head over to Cava Azul Cocina & Cantina in Woodin Creek for scratch-made regional plates and a selection from their massive tequila menu, or grab a gourmet cookie from Crumbl Cookies for a late-night treat. As the sun sets, the outdoor lawns and patio fire pits across town fill up with neighbors lingering late into the warm August night.

### **EP9**

September introduces a crisp edge to the morning air, signaling the start of grape harvest and crush season. Even though local farms do not grow the grapes, the energy across the town's four distinct districts is electric as tons of freshly picked fruit arrive from Eastern Washington. It is the peak season for regional winemaking, giving the entire valley an industrious, celebratory mood as production gets into full swing.

Take advantage of a crisp September morning with a classic river walk. Park at Wilmot Gateway Park and stroll south along the Sammamish River Trail as the autumn light sharpens over the valley. After taking in the fall air, head into downtown for a hot espresso and fresh pastries from Mercurys Coffee Co, or stop by Heritage Restaurant|Bar for pan-seared trout, cheddar biscuits, and a glass of local Washington wine.

Understanding how the city is laid out makes navigating harvest season much easier. The city is organized into four distinct zones: the tourist-heavy Hollywood District, the commercial West Valley downtown core, the industrial Warehouse District, and the quiet rural residential neighborhoods of Wellington. Keeping those boundaries in mind helps you choose whether you want high-energy hospitality or a quiet, low-key afternoon.

As September progresses, the annual salmon run begins along the Sammamish River banks. Watching Chinook and Coho salmon make their way upstream under the shadow of colorful fall foliage is a captivating free seasonal ritual. Standing along the quiet riverbanks at Woodin Creek Park, watching the fish move through the clear water, connects you directly to the natural rhythms that have defined this valley for thousands of years.

### **EP10**

October brings brilliant fall foliage, damp forest trails, and early dusk across the river basin. The city's master planning continues to preserve its agricultural roots, keeping commercial development contained while protecting large natural pockets like Gold Creek County Park. As leaves turn brilliant shades of orange and red, the forest canopy transforms into a vivid autumn landscape.

Embrace the cozy October mood with a visit to Bosk Brew Works in the Warehouse District for a crisp lager, or head to The Hollywood Tavern for a bowl of warm, hearty soup beside their outdoor fire pit. Afterward, head over to Gold Creek County Park for a peaceful autumn walk along the quiet forest dirt loops, enjoying the crisp air and rustling leaves without any of the crowds found in the commercial areas.

As daylight fades earlier each afternoon, managing daily routines becomes all about efficiency. Commercial errands along NE 175th Street remain fast and convenient, allowing you to stock up on household goods before the evening rain sets in. If the weather turns exceptionally wet, pivot indoors to the cozy fireside seating at Purple Cafe or Willows Lodge to dry off and enjoy a quiet afternoon.

The town's seasonal rhythm slows down gracefully in October, shifting focus toward community gatherings and autumn comfort. Local families drop by DeYoung Park to admire the historic feed mill scale under falling yellow leaves, while craft beverage spots release seasonal brews and cozy autumn drink rotations. It is a picturesque time of year that balances rustic Pacific Northwest scenery with warm neighborhood hospitality.

### **EP11**

November brings the first real winter storms, early darkness at 4:30 PM, and the steady sound of rain on tin roofs. With the harvest complete, local wineries turn their attention to post-harvest barrel tastings and quiet cellar work. The summer crowds are long gone, leaving the valley in a peaceful winter hibernation that locals thoroughly cherish.

When the winter rain starts pouring, head to Heritage Restaurant|Bar for a warm, comfortable meal of regional seafood and warm cheddar biscuits. Afterward, retreat to the cozy Fireside Lounge at Willows Lodge, where you can sit near the massive stone fireplace, sip a glass of Washington red, and watch the rain fall on the surrounding gardens.

Navigating town in November is smooth and hassle-free, with short commute times and plenty of parking across all four districts. Getting daily errands done at Target, Haggen, or the Post Office takes just minutes, leaving you with plenty of time to enjoy a quiet, low-stimulus afternoon away from the rain.

The rich history of the area feels especially present on dark November evenings. From the ancestral Snoqualmie Tribe canoe routes along Squak Slough to the 19th-century logging mills and the historic 1910s DeYoung produce packing shed, this valley has always been defined by hard work and resilience through long Pacific Northwest winters. Sitting warm indoors while the wind blows through the valley trees, you appreciate the deep sense of shelter and community that defines life here.

### **EP12**

December arrives with festive holiday lights, cool winter air, and a warm community spirit across the valley. Woodin Creek Village transforms into a holiday destination, featuring sparkling light displays, curbless pedestrian strolls, and seasonal pop-up markets. The town strikes a perfect balance between holiday cheer and relaxed, small-town comfort.

Spend a festive December afternoon exploring Woodin Creek Plaza. Stop by Seatown Sweets or Crumbl Cookies for custom holiday treats, then grab a warm specialty latte from Luv Market & Cafe or a hot drink from Bobae Coffee & Tea. Take a relaxed evening walk through the illuminated plaza walkways, enjoying the festive lights before heading to Cava Azul for a warm Mexican dinner.

Holiday shopping in December is surprisingly stress-free compared to major urban malls. The city's density of local wineries, craft breweries like Watts Brewing, and boutique shops in Woodin Creek makes picking up regional gifts simple and enjoyable. You can gather local wines, artisanal goods, and specialty coffee beans all within a short drive across downtown.

As the year comes to a close, the city showcases its journey from a 19th-century homesteading and logging settlement along Squak Slough into a premier craft beverage capital. Stand on the Wilmot River Bridge on a cold December evening, watching the river reflect the holiday lights while distant wood smoke rises into the crisp night air. It is a scene that captures the enduring warmth, rich history, and welcoming spirit of life in the valley.
