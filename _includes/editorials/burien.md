### **EP1**

Long before Burien officially incorporated back in 1993, this stretch was defined by quiet homesteading and working-class grit. German immigrant Gottlieb Burian threw down his log cabin on Lake Burien in 1884, setting off a slow transformation from logging camp to summer hideaway. That steady, unpretentious spirit still lingers when the post-holiday January haze rolls off the water, keeping the city grounded while the rest of the region rushes into the new year.

For a quiet two-hour reset, grab a warm treat from Orange Bakery on SW 153rd St and wander down historic SW 152nd St. Slip into the Highline Heritage Museum at 8th Ave SW to look through local artifacts in the old civic library building, then wrap up your stroll with a casual, warming lunch at Pigfish Cafe or Burien Fish House down the block.

Daily life here means balancing high walkability in the core with pure car dependency everywhere else. While you can easily walk between bakeries and city services along SW 152nd St, heading out for bigger errands requires firing up the engine. If you are catching the morning commuter rush when Highway 509 Northbound bottlenecks, skip the main line and cut over using Des Moines Memorial Drive S or 8th Ave SW to hit Highway 99 without destroying your sanity.

January brings low fog across the saltwater shorelines, carrying a deep stillness back to the ancestral lands of the Duwamish people, who gathered along these shores for generations. If you need to dodge the damp chill, skip the crowds and head up to Mathison Park for a quiet view of winter flight paths and Mount Rainier. It is a slow, reflective season that rewards those who know where to find a warm bench and a clear view of the Sound.

### **EP2**

Burien has always possessed a low-key, creative streak tucked behind its suburban residential streets. This is the town where Jerry Cantrell and Mike Starr pounded out early Alice in Chains riffs in a neighborhood home, and where Mudhoney founder Mark Arm walked the halls of Highline High School. That underground energy feels right at home during February, when grey skies force everyone indoors to swap stories and find creative warmth.

Beat the late-winter chill by heading downtown to Lowrider Cookie Company on SW 153rd St for a fresh sweet treat. Take your box west along SW 152nd St, popping into independent shops before finding tucked-in seating at Elliott Bay Brewing for a slow lunch in a cozy booth.

Downtown parking requires a little strategy when rain drives everyone to the same block. Free two-hour street spots along SW 152nd St go quickly during the lunch rush, but locals bypass the street hunting entirely by parking in the free underground garage at Burien Town Square off 5th Ave SW. It keeps your car dry and gets you out of the weather in seconds.

When heavy cloud cover settles low over the valley, the multicultural corridor along Ambaum Blvd S comes alive with the aroma of hot soups, fresh panaderias, and local spots like Tacos El Hass. It is a reminder of how rich and layered this city is beyond its central strip. On dark February evenings, hiding out near a warm kitchen feels less like surviving winter and more like enjoying a well-kept secret.

### **EP3**

When the Lake Burien Railway electric streetcar line kicked off service in 1912, it transformed a distant logging woods into a popular weekend cottage getaway. That spirit of sudden movement returns every March, as long winter hibernations give way to sudden bursts of energy whenever the sun manages to poke through the spring squalls.

Start your afternoon with a coffee from Fivestar Coffee over on International Blvd before heading down to Seahurst Park. Stroll the hard-packed gravel Shoreline Trail taking in sharp west-facing views across Puget Sound toward Vashon Island and the Olympic Mountains, then loop up through the quiet, shaded North Forest Trail for a green canopy reset.

Early spring weather changes quickly, which means parking lots fill without warning on the first clear afternoon. The lower beachfront lot at Seahurst Park reaches capacity fast on nice days, so hitting the park early or being prepared for the steep ten-minute walk down from the upper overflow lot is essential. Keep a pair of sturdy walking shoes in the trunk for the climb back up.

March brings sharp, shifting light across the saltwater, reflecting off driftwood logs and wet gravel paths. These shorelines carry the long history of the Duwamish people, honored by tribal figures like Catherine Troeh who preserved local heritage for future generations. Watching sudden sunbeams break over the water from a quiet bench at Mathison Park makes the cool March winds completely worth it.

Spend an hour browsing the seasonal Thursday Burien Farmers Market vendors before walking down SW 152nd St for an outdoor craft pint on the patio at Logan Brewing Company.

Daily logistics along the Ambaum Blvd S and 1st Ave S corridors mean mixing walkable bakery trips with high-speed commercial traffic. When morning delays stack up on 1st Ave S heading north, cutting through 8th Ave SW offers a steady alternative route through quiet residential neighborhoods, getting you around the signal delays without stress.

Spring daylight brings fresh energy to the international corridors along 1st Ave S and Ambaum Blvd S, where banh mi delis and Halal grocers share space with neighborhood markets. The flat, wide concrete sidewalks in the core make moving around easy for everyone, offering an accessible space to soak up the early sun before heading home as dusk settles over the Sound.

### **EP5**

That shift from heavy labor to community gathering mirrors Burien's own evolution over the decades. By May, that transition feels complete as long evenings draw people out of their homes and into public spaces.

Grab a casual lunch at Red Wagon Burger on SW 148th St, then take the short drive over to Lake Burien Park for an afternoon under the trees. Follow up with a stroll through the quiet neighborhood streets to spot historic summer cottages from the electric streetcar days, ending your loop with a cold drink back downtown.

May brings higher traffic to local parks, making arrival timing key. If you are heading out for an evening loop at Mathison Park on S 146th St, keep in mind that street parking near small green spaces fills up with dog walkers right around five o'clock. Arriving forty-five minutes later gives you open spots and clear views of Mount Rainier in the warm evening light.

Pre-summer twilights in Burien linger late into the night, casting a warm golden glow across the Olympic Range. The air carries the fresh scent of saltwater and blooming gardens. Sitting on a park bench watching planes descend toward Sea-Tac while the mountain glows in the distance is one of those simple, unscripted local moments that define the season.

### **EP6**

June delivers the longest daylight of the year, with twilight stretching late past nine at night. It is a time of open local celebration, shared outdoor spaces, and endless summer light.

### **EP7**

Long before modern beachgoers laid out towels along Seahurst Beach, the Duwamish people used these exact shorelines for seasonal shellfish gathering and summer fishing. Today, that deep connection to Puget Sound remains central to Burien's summer identity, bringing generations of residents down to the water whenever July temperatures spike.

Beat the heat by picking up takeout from Birrieria La Santa Tacos & More on Des Moines Memorial Drive, then driving straight down to Seahurst Park. Walk along the gravel beach, explore the intertidal marine life exposed by negative low tides at the Seahurst Marine Reserve, and find a flat piece of driftwood to enjoy lunch while watching ferries cross the Sound.

Mid-summer weekends at Seahurst Park test everyone's patience if you do not plan ahead. The lower beachfront parking lot regularly fills up before nine-thirty in the morning. Arriving by eight-thirty guarantees a spot right at the seawall, saving you from hauling heavy gear up and down the steep forest trail in high heat.

High summer in Burien revolves entirely around the water. The heat is tempered by steady marine breezes that rustle through the coastal woods. Evenings are best spent on a view bench watching the sun dip behind the distant peaks of the Olympic Range, surrounded by the gentle lap of low waves on the gravel shore.
