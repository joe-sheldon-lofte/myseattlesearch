### **EP1**

January in Clyde Hill brings a crisp, quiet calm across the hilltop. Low winter fog settles over Lake Washington while the skyline of Seattle drifts in and out of view from the higher ridges. The air feels clear and sharp, giving local residential avenues a peaceful quality after the holiday rush. Longtime residents like Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella or former Mariners star Félix Hernández likely appreciate this winter stillness, where leafy streets sit quiet under gray skies.

Long before high-profile tech leaders and sports figures moved to these hilltops, the area was traditional territory for the Duwamish and Snoqualmie Coast Salish people, known locally as the Lake People or hah-chu-AHBSH. Later, in 1882, an Irish immigrant named Patrick Downey built a simple one-story log cabin near 100th Ave NE and NE 12th St before venturing further toward Meydenbauer Bay. By 1953, local property owners voted to incorporate specifically to prevent commercial development, enacting a strict 20,000-square-foot minimum lot size to keep the area purely residential.

If you are looking for an unscripted morning outing, start with the Crest View Walk and Bakery Loop. Pick up fresh morning pastries and pour-over coffee at North of Main on 112th Ave NE, then stroll west along quiet residential NE 24th St. Walk all the way to Muromoto Memorial Park at 92nd Ave NE and NE 26th St to catch open views across Lake Washington, before returning through the quiet tree-lined path at Clyde Hill Park.

Daily life here requires adjusting to a few practical realities. You will not be walking to grab a carton of milk because there is not a single commercial storefront within city limits. Essential grocery runs require a brief three-minute drive down 104th Ave NE into Downtown Bellevue or nearby QFC hubs. During morning commute hours, northbound 84th Ave NE backs up severely toward SR 520, so smart drivers cut east along NE 14th St to 92nd Ave NE or 98th Ave NE to reach NE 24th St without sitting in line.

### **EP2**

February brings a quiet winter lull to Clyde Hill. Gray skies hang low, and light rains turn the evergreen canopy dark and glossy. This is a month for slowing down, putting on rain jackets, and taking tranquil walks along residential shoulder paths while winter clouds roll across the Puget Sound region.

These quiet residential slopes carry a rich agricultural history that surprises many modern newcomers. Before World War II, Japanese immigrant farmers cleared local forest land and transformed Clyde Hill into a famous strawberry growing hub. Their hard work was celebrated regionally at the Bellevue Strawberry Festival from 1925 until 1942, creating an agricultural legacy that still echoes across the landscape today.

On a drizzly afternoon, duck out for hot comfort food at Manager's Pizza on 12th Ave NE or grab a sweet treat from Rosie Bakery. Afterward, take a peaceful stroll through the low-stimulus wooded loop trail at Clyde Hill Park right behind City Hall. The dense evergreen canopy keeps off the worst of the drizzle, providing a calm natural sanctuary away from traffic noise.

Navigating the hill in winter requires a bit of practical know-how. The town features no commercial parking structures or meters anywhere, so public parking is strictly limited to civic lots near City Hall or street shoulders that strictly enforce distance from the curb. The steep hillside topography makes bicycle riding a serious challenge, so most neighbors rely on cars to run quick errands down the hill into Downtown Bellevue.
