### **EP1**

Long before homesteaders built the first sawmill in the 1890s and called this ridge Mill Town, the Puyallup Tribe (known as the puyaləpabš) fished and camped along the Hylebos Creek watershed. When residents applied for an official post office in 1905, the federal government pointed out another Washington town had already claimed Mill Town, forcing a quick pivot to honor the poet John Milton before incorporating in 1907. That quiet independence still defines the community today, as strict low density zoning keeps industrial sprawl along the Pierce and King County border at arm's length.

On a crisp January morning, kick off a post holiday reset with a warm drink or fresh blend at I'm Juiced on Pioneer before heading west into Milton Community Park. Take a slow loop around the upper fields, watch the locals at Milltown Commons Skatepark, and step directly onto the paved Milton Interurban Trail. Following this historical right-of-way toward 70th Avenue gives you a calm two-hour outing without needing to fight for a spot on a trail head.

Living here means getting comfortable with your car keys, because you are not walking to pick up a week of groceries. Quick convenience runs happen along Milton Way, but major market trips require driving north toward Federal Way on Pacific Highway East or south into Fife. When peak hour traffic piles up at the corner of Meridian Avenue East and Milton Way, smart drivers skip the choke point entirely by using 70th Avenue East or Porter Way to cross between towns.

Midwinter brings thick blankets of valley fog that settle over the lowlands while the ridge stays quiet. If you want a clear perspective on a cold afternoon, drive up to Hill Tower Park right beside the city water tower for a wide view over the Puyallup Valley. The post holiday hush feels right at home among these neighborhood streets, where quiet cafes and rain glazed roads mark the start of the year.

### **EP2**

Between 1902 and 1928, the Puget Sound Electric Railway connected Seattle and Tacoma directly through this ridge, triggering an early real estate rush. The Milton Land Company pitched plots for as little as twenty-nine dollars an acre, drawing families who wanted a rural home within easy reach of urban factory shifts. You can still feel that electric railway legacy as you move through town today, where historic rights-of-way outline modern residential blocks.

Beat the midwinter gray by taking a quiet hike through West Milton Nature Preserve off 28th Avenue. Follow the canopy-shaded dirt paths along Sweetwater Creek, where the rushing water drowns out the noise of nearby commuter corridors. Afterwards, make the short drive down to Pacific Highway East to warm up with fresh baked pastries from Bon Appetina's or grab tacos at Señor Taco.

Navigating residential parking during wet winter months can test your patience, especially near the narrow roads surrounding Surprise Lake. Local drivers know to leave their vehicles in the free dedicated civic parking lots at Milton Community Park off 15th Avenue or Laurel Street instead of crowding neighborhood shoulders. It saves you the trouble of tight turnarounds on slick, unpaved edges.

February brings endless gray skies and soft drizzle that makes staying indoors particularly appealing. On the worst weather days, grabbing a hot espresso from Good Vibes Espresso and driving down to the Fife Library offers a quiet refuge. The muted winter light sitting over the Puyallup Valley serves as a peaceful reminder of the town's slow paced rhythm.

### **EP3**

Long before modern homes ringed its shoreline, Surprise Lake was homesteaded by William G. Morse in 1890 under the name Morse Lake. By 1903, the State Spiritualist Association of Washington established Camp Edgewood on its banks, gathering believers from across the region for summer séances and spiritual retreats. That curious mix of rustic retreat and eccentric local history gives the lake an origin story unlike anywhere else in the valley.

March offers sudden bursts of spring sunshine that invite you back outdoors for a simple two-hour excursion. Start with a quiet stroll through West Milton Nature Preserve, watching for early songbirds returning to the canopy along the East Branch of Hylebos Creek. Finish your outing with a brief drive to Olympic View Park, where the vantage point provides an open lookout across the greening Puyallup Valley toward Mount Rainier.

Daily errands require a clear strategy if you want to keep your travel times short as spring activity picks up. Local retail is scattered, so running out for hardware or major grocery hauls usually means hopping onto Pacific Highway South toward Federal Way or Edgewood. Avoiding the primary intersection at Meridian Avenue East by cutting through Porter Way saves precious minutes during the early evening rush.

As the weather shifts between sudden rain squalls and bright blue skies, the landscape comes alive with deep green buds. When afternoon light breaks through the clouds, folks head over to Off Camber Brewing or Rail Hop'n Brewing to enjoy a cold local drink. Late afternoon light hitting the valley floor makes this changing season feel hopeful and grounded.

### **EP4**

Shaping a small town squeezed between expanding industrial hubs requires deliberate civic planning. Leaders like S. Kenneth Simmons and former Mayor Katrina Asay helped establish strict landscape buffers and low density residential policies that define the modern streetscape. These green corridor rules ensure the community retains its open feel even as neighboring regional logistics centers expand along the county line.

When April brings daylight stretching past seven o'clock, grab an espresso from Gibs Coffee or head north toward JSE Coffee - Twin lakes before visiting Milton Community Park. Known locally as Triangle Park, its open fields and paved walking paths are perfect for an easy afternoon loop. You can seamlessly transition onto the Interurban Trail, enjoying the fresh blossoms lining the old railway track.

Living here means accepting that running daily errands requires a set of car keys rather than a pair of walking shoes. While basic items are easy to grab along Milton Way, substantial trips require driving into nearby Fife or Puyallup. To avoid getting caught in bumper to bumper backups along Meridian, local drivers use 70th Avenue East as a smooth connection between neighborhoods.

April brings a soft energy as lawns green up and fruit trees bloom across older homestead lots. Sitting on a park bench while breezes move off the Puget Sound tributaries brings a welcome pause to a busy week. The mild spring air makes every outdoor moment feel like a long awaited exhale after winter.

### **EP5**

Long before homesteaders cleared timber on the hill, the Muckleshoot Indian Tribe and Puyallup Tribe used the streams and rivers here for seasonal camps and salmon fishing. The waterways connecting Hylebos Creek to the Puyallup River served as vital travel routes and harvesting grounds for generations. Recognizing this deep ancestral footprint offers a rich sense of history whenever you walk near the local creeks today.

On a warm May evening, start your routine with a fresh juice from I'm Juiced on Pioneer before heading down to Triangle Park. Spend time watching local skaters practice tricks at Milltown Commons Skatepark before taking a quiet walk along the paved Milton Interurban Trail. The path stretches out smoothly toward 70th Avenue, making it ideal for a relaxing pre-dinner stroll.

If you want to enjoy the Interurban Trail without company, the key unwritten rule is getting out early. Hit the path before nine o'clock on weekend mornings to catch quiet bird watching conditions before dog walkers and joggers arrive. Parking in the free lot near City Hall off 15th Avenue guarantees you avoid congested street parking near residential quarters.

May ushers in long twilights where the sun hangs over the horizon well into the evening hours. The lingering light invites people out onto front porches and garden beds to clear winter debris. There is a palpable shift in the air as neighbors greet each other along sidewalk loops under golden skies.

### **EP6**

The transition from a rugged timber settlement to a connected suburban community was fueled by the Puget Sound Electric Railway in the early twentieth century. Before cars dominated daily life, residents relied on electric trains to commute between Seattle and Tacoma, creating an active rural stop atop the ridge. That historical transit corridor remains embedded in the physical layout of the city today.

Escape the midday June heat by heading into the deep canopy of West Milton Nature Preserve. The shaded dirt paths along Sweetwater Creek offer a cool, low stimulus sanctuary where you can hear spawning trout splashing in the current. After your trail walk, head over to Rail Hop'n Brewing or check out Top Down Brewing Co for a cold brew and pizza.

Living near Pacific Highway East gives you quick access to an impressive multicultural corridor of family owned eateries. When you do not feel like cooking, you can easily grab street tacos at Taqueria Los Pericos or hit local food trucks like The Frying Dutchman for fish and chips. Commuting along these arterial routes requires patience, but the diverse food options make the trip worthwhile.

June brings the summer solstice and nearly sixteen hours of daylight to the Pacific Northwest. The evening air smells of fresh cut grass and cedar trees as families gather in backyards and public green spaces. Watching the sky slowly turn pink and purple over the valley floor is a classic seasonal ritual.

### **EP7**

Through the mid twentieth century, Surprise Lake operated as a beloved regional getaway known as Surprise Lake Resort until it closed in 1975. Families from across Pierce and King counties flooded the shores for swimming, boat rentals, and summer picnics. While the old resort facilities are long gone, the lake's history as a hot weather destination remains a cherished piece of local lore.

Take advantage of a sunny July afternoon by planning a simple two-hour outing to Olympic View Park. Bring a folding chair and enjoy clear views stretching across the Puyallup Valley toward Mount Rainier sitting massive on the horizon. Afterward, make a trip over to Crawfish Island or captain's Seafood for a casual seafood feast.

Summer brings extra visitor traffic around Surprise Lake, making parking on surrounding residential streets particularly tricky. Driving down narrow lanes looking for a spot often leads to dead ends, so parking at the free city lot off Laurel Street near Triangle Park is always the smarter choice. From there, you can walk or ride a bike without stressing over tight street parking.

July brings peak summer warmth, with temperatures reaching into the eighties and dry mountain breezes clearing the air. Long golden hour afternoons draw residents to outdoor benches and shaded park gazebos. As dusk settles, the cool saltwater breeze from Puget Sound drifts inland, making for comfortable summer nights.

### **EP8**

Mid-August marks the highlight of the community calendar with the annual Milton Days festival and its iconic parade down Milton Way. Covering just half a mile but taking nearly an hour to finish, it holds the legendary local title of the longest and shortest parade in the state. Neighbors line the sidewalks to watch classic cars, marching bands, and community floats roll past at a joyful crawling pace.

Spend a weekend afternoon immersed in the festivities by grabbing a cold drink at Good Vibes Espresso or a fresh smoothie at I'm Juiced on Pioneer. Walk down to Milton Community Park to inspect the classic car show, check out vendor booths, and catch up with neighbors under the shade trees. It is an unscripted afternoon that captures the authentic, unpretentious spirit of the town.

Parade weekend means major traffic adjustments along Milton Way, so planning your routes is essential. Commuters looking to travel between Fife and Federal Way during the event should strictly rely on Porter Way or 70th Avenue East to avoid closed roads. Parking at the City Hall lots off 15th Avenue early in the day guarantees you a spot before crowds arrive.

Late August brings a distinctive golden haze to the valley as the summer harvest reaches its peak. Warm afternoons give way to comfortable evenings where you can watch the sunset from the high ground at Hill Tower Park. As summer begins to wind down, the quiet rhythm of the neighborhood returns.

### **EP10**

The Puyallup and Muckleshoot peoples relied on these freshwater tributaries for autumn harvesting and traditional knowledge passed down over generations. That biological legacy continues every autumn as migrating fish return to spawn in local streams.

### **EP11**

When residents chose to rename Mill Town after the English poet John Milton in 1905, they were looking for an identity that would set them apart from other Puget Sound logging camps. Incorporating two years later in 1907, the town established a legacy of independent civic governance that survived the decline of the timber industry. That resilient history underpins the tight knit community spirit that persists today.

On a rainy November day, swing through the drive-thru at Good Vibes Espresso or order a warm specialty brew at JSE Coffee - Twin lakes. Take your hot drink up to the high ground at Hill Tower Park, where you can sit comfortably in your car and watch winter storm systems roll across the wide Puyallup Valley. It is an unscripted local ritual that turns gray weather into a dramatic spectator sport.

November brings early darkness around four thirty in the afternoon, making evening driving along unlit residential streets more demanding. Commuter traffic at the intersection of Meridian Avenue East and Milton Way becomes particularly congested during wet evening commutes. Wrapped in heavy coats and damp scarves, neighbors pass each other with quick, friendly nods in local coffee spots.
