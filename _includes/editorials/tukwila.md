### **EP1**

Long before high-volume commercial corridors carved through the valley floor, this stretch of ground was defined by thick hazelnut groves clinging to the banks of the Black and Duwamish rivers. The Coast Salish peoples named the location after those native trees, using the word t'əqʷila to describe a place of gathering and natural abundance. That original identity still anchors the community today, reminding anyone paying attention that under all the industrial pavement beats the steady heart of a historic river valley.

On a quiet post-holiday morning, start your day with a warm plate of East African Sambusas and a steaming cup of spiced chai at Spice Bridge Food Hall on Tukwila International Blvd. Once you have warmed up, take a short drive down to Tukwila Pond Park for a peaceful walk along the shaded wetland loop. Watching blue herons glide over the calm water as the morning mist rises off the pond provides a surprisingly tranquil reset before the rest of the world wakes up.

Getting around town in the depth of winter requires knowing how to bypass commercial bottlenecks. When interstate traffic jams up near the retail core, locals head north along Tukwila International Blvd or slip onto 42nd Ave S to keep moving. For daily grocery runs, skipping the large suburban parking lots in favor of smaller international grocers along the main boulevard saves both time and sanity.

January brings a calm, introspective mood to the valley floor as low hanging fog clings to the surrounding hills. It is a season for ducking inside warm spaces, watching winter light soften the landscape, and honoring the quiet stillness of the post-holiday lull.

### **EP2**

Historical roots run deep around the old core of the valley, where early leaders like Joseph Foster established homesteads near Foster's Landing and helped build the region's first schools. Down on 144th St, the Old Tukwila City Hall from 1920 now serves as an archive run by the Tukwila Historical Society, preserving stories from the era when steam-powered riverboats traded along the waterways. Remembering that pioneer past offers a great perspective on how an agricultural trading post transformed into a regional crossroads.

A cold February afternoon calls for an unscripted outing starting at 85C Bakery Cafe for fresh sweet bread and coffee before heading over to Westfield Southcenter to walk the sprawling indoor concourses. Alternatively, step inside Old Tukwila City Hall to browse local history exhibits before picking up hot soup at a cafe along Tukwila International Blvd. Either option keeps you warm while allowing you to trace the town's living timeline.

Finding parking near major retail hubs during winter weekends can test anyone's patience. The insider move is to avoid the packed multi-level garages altogether and target the open surface space near Southcenter Plaza or use commuter spaces at the Tukwila Sounder Station. That small adjustment eliminates loop after loop of stressful parking deck hunting.

Late winter in the valley carries a heavy gray canopy that makes indoor sanctuaries essential. Whether you are finding a cozy corner reading table at the King County Library Tukwila Branch or watching rain streak down cafe windows, February is about embracing low-key warmth while winter slowly plays itself out.

### **EP3**

Prominent Northwest School painter William Cumming spent years living in Tukwila, capturing the unique intersection of rural beauty and industrial grit that defines the valley. His artwork celebrated working-class life against the backdrop of changing light, a visual rhythm that you can still feel today along the river corridors. His legacy reminds us that art lives in everyday routines and industrial landscapes, not just in sterile museum galleries.

As early spring begins to tease the Pacific Northwest, grab a cup of coffee at Dubai Cafeteria & Expresso on Tukwila International Blvd. From there, head down to Fort Dent Park and set out on a two-hour footpath journey along the Green River Trail heading south toward Bicentennial Park. The walk offers wide views of swollen river waters reflecting sudden patches of blue sky, ending with a quiet break on a riverside view bench.

Spring brings a surge of youth sports activity to local fields, which can complicate your morning routine if you do not time it right. To secure free parking and enjoy quiet trail loops at Fort Dent Park, make sure to arrive before 9:00 AM on weekend mornings before regional soccer and rugby leagues claim the complex.

March delivers rapid weather shifts, moving from sudden sun squalls to crisp spring breezes within minutes. Cascade snowmelt fills the river channel, green shoots emerge from the damp soil, and the valley starts to shake off its winter sleep.

### **EP4**

Transportation history is written into the path of Interurban Avenue, where electric commuter trains ran along the Seattle-Tacoma Interurban Railway from 1902 to 1928. The town was also home to the first Macadam-paved road in Washington state, proving its long legacy as a crucial regional connection point. Today, that old electric rail corridor lives on as a fourteen-mile paved trail enjoyed by cyclists and walkers alike.

Take advantage of an April afternoon by stopping at Paris Baguette on Southcenter Pkwy for fresh pastries before embarking on a spring walk along the Green River Trail. You can follow the paved path past blossoming trees, or head over to Tukwila Pond Park to walk the shaded circuit and watch returning waterfowl. Crossing the pedestrian skybridge into the Southcenter riverfront promenade makes for an easy, rewarding afternoon loop.

Daily commuting gets much smoother when you utilize public transit options scattered through the city. Taking advantage of level platform boarding at the Tukwila Link Light Rail station lets you bypass regional freeway backups completely when heading north toward Seattle.

April brings a tangible awakening as daylight stretches past 7:30 PM across the valley floor. Cherry blossoms bloom along the paved trails, neighbors gather outdoors again, and patio coffee stops replace huddled indoor coffee runs.

### **EP5**

Tukwila has a fascinating knack for subtle pop culture history, including the famous 1981 story of real estate developer Mario Segale. When Nintendo of America leased a local warehouse from him, a heated confrontation over overdue rent led creator Shigeru Miyamoto to name their flagship video game plumber Mario after Segale. That unpretentious, straightforward commercial history is classic valley culture.

Spend a late May evening picking up tacos at Aceituno's Mexican Food on Southcenter Pkwy before heading over to Fort Dent Park. After enjoying your meal on a picnic table, take a casual loop around the perimeter trails and watch evening sports matches under the bright stadium lights at Starfire Sports.

Rush hour between 4:00 PM and 6:00 PM can turn main arterial roads into standstills, especially around Southcenter Parkway near I-5 and I-405. Smart drivers bypass the gridlock by cutting north along Interurban Ave S or using 42nd Ave S to travel toward Tukwila International Blvd.

May brings long, golden twilights and an infectious pre-summer energy. Cool evening breezes blow off the Green River, drawing runners, dog walkers, and families out into the fresh air until dusk finally settles.

### **EP6**

For generations, Coast Salish tribes gathered at this junction, relying on the rich natural ecosystem for sustenance and community. Today, standing near that historic confluence offers a moment of reflection on how much the physical landscape has changed while the river's spirit endures.

Mark the summer solstice with a trip to Spice Bridge Food Hall to grab fresh East African dishes, then head over to Bicentennial Park. Walk along the Green River Trail toward the historic river confluence, finding a peaceful bench to sit and watch the river glide past under endless twilight skies.

As summer traffic picks up, navigating commercial zones requires strategic planning. If you are heading out for weekend shopping, avoid the central parking garages by using outer ring parking lots near Southcenter Plaza, or leave the car entirely and bike the paved Interurban Trail.

June brings peak solstice energy to the valley with lingering daylight stretching toward 10:00 PM. Riverside picnics, evening bike rides, and maritime breezes off the sound make this one of the most delightful months to explore the outdoors.
