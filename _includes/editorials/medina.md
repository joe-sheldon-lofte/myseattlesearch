### **EP1**

Long before tech moguls claimed these shorelines, this point on Lake Washington was defined by quiet fruit orchards, berry farms, and strawberry fields connected to Seattle by steam ferries. Dabney C. Point named the settlement after Medina, Saudi Arabia in 1891, though the town did not officially incorporate until 1955. Today, high hedges line Evergreen Point Road, concealing sprawling modern estates like Bill Gates's Xanadu 2.0 and Jeff Bezos's massive waterfront property. Yet the town's agricultural roots linger quietly beneath its ultra-wealthy exterior, where strict zoning keeps commercial expansion entirely at bay.

January brings low fog off Lake Washington, creating a perfect backdrop for a quiet morning outing. Start by grabbing a warm drink or light bite at the local market near Evergreen Point Road. From there, walk north along the tree-canopied sidewalk of Evergreen Point Road past deep green hedges until you reach Medina Beach Park at 501 Evergreen Point Road. Walk out onto the public swimming dock to spend an hour taking in panoramic views of the water and the Seattle skyline shrouded in winter mist.

Living here or spending time on these streets means adjusting to a unique set of daily rules. You will not find a grocery store, a coffee shop drive-thru, or a retail shopping strip anywhere within city limits. Grabbing basic kitchen essentials or running errands requires a five to ten minute drive east into Downtown Bellevue or Clyde Hill. Furthermore, automated license plate reading security cameras monitor every entry point into town, and unauthorized street parking or slow cruising often invites swift police check-ins.

This shoreline carries deeper history as the ancestral territory of the Southern Lushootseed-speaking Coast Salish peoples, including the Duwamish and Muckleshoot tribes, who navigated these eastern bays for seasonal fishing and canoe travel. On crisp winter afternoons, head over to Medina Park at NE 12th St and 82nd Ave NE. Walking around the dual duck ponds under the fog-draped canopy offers a serene, low-stimulus reset far removed from highway noise.

### **EP2**

Medina has maintained a strict R-20 residential zoning code for decades, requiring lot sizes of at least 20,000 square feet and keeping commercial operations almost entirely nonexistent. Only a tiny handful of historic exemptions survive, such as Overlake Golf & Country Club, the village post office building, and Wells Medina Nursery on NE 24th St. Designated an official Tree City USA since 2006, the neighborhood treats its towering canopy and quiet residential lanes as its primary civic identity.

February is an ideal time to seek inspiration for upcoming garden projects before the spring rush. Head over to Wells Medina Nursery on NE 24th St to wander through the covered display greenhouses while winter rain taps on the roof. Afterwards, take a brisk shoreline stroll along Evergreen Point Road down to Medina Beach Park. This public waterfront site is a quirky civic hybrid, combining the public beach grounds directly with Medina City Hall and the Medina Police Department headquarters on a historic former ferry landing.

Navigating Medina requires precise awareness of local parking restrictions. Street parking along Evergreen Point Road and Overlake Drive is strictly limited or banned altogether, and public spaces are confined to small lots at the parks. City officials even installed permanent steel bollards at Viewpoint Park off Overlake Drive to block angled parking stalls, eliminating dangerous reverse backups onto the narrow roadway. Keep your car in designated park lots to avoid a swift citation.

When the gray skies hang heavy over the lake, locals pivot eastward for warmth and food. While there are no dining strips in town, grabbing a hot espresso or treat at Medina Coffeeroasters Spotlight in downtown Medina or taking a short drive to Bellevue spots provides a cozy refuge. Ending the afternoon at Fairweather Park gives you a front row seat to moody winter sunsets, where choppy gray water reflects the fading daylight across Lake Washington.
