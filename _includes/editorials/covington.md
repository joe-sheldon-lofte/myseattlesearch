### **EP1**

January in Covington carries a quiet, damp stillness, with low fog clinging to the dense forest canopy around Jenkins Creek. Before it became a suburban hub, this landscape was known as Jenkins Prairie, a name that eventually gave way to Covington in 1900 when Northern Pacific Railway surveyor Richard Covington helped construct the Palmer Cutoff bypass. Walking through town on a frosty morning, you can almost hear the faint echo of steam trains that once cut through these timber stands. Long before those tracks were laid, Whulshootseed-speaking Coast Salish peoples, including ancestors of the Muckleshoot Indian Tribe, gathered seasonal plants and fished along these exact creeks.

For a perfect two-hour winter reset, start by grabbing a hot espresso at Farmhouse Brew on 3rd Avenue to take the edge off the morning chill. Carry your warm cup over to Jenkins Creek Park off SE 267th Place, where twenty-three acres of quiet public trails wander under a canopy of native cedar trees. The dirt path softens underfoot as fog hovers low over the stream beds, offering a calm, sensory-quiet sanctuary far removed from daily noise. It is an unscripted local ritual that resets your mind before the workday kicks into gear.

Getting around here in midwinter requires a practical mindset because daily errands almost always demand four wheels. With major retail destinations packed together along the State Route 516 corridor, catching a green light on Kent-Kangley Road during peak hours takes real patience. Local commuters know to dodge the main gridlock near the highway by cutting down Wax Road or taking 180th Avenue SE to navigate northern residential neighborhoods. If you need to make a quick grocery run without getting stuck in retail traffic, taking these back paths saves precious time.

As dusk settles early over the Cascade foothill tree canopies, the town turns inward toward warm kitchens and quiet living rooms. The damp winter air carries the crisp smell of woodsmoke from neighborhood chimneys, reminding you of the town's rugged logging roots. While the commercial strip along Kent-Kangley stays busy with shoppers, the residential pockets remain peacefully dark and calm. It is a slow, reflective month that lets the city catch its breath before spring arrives.

### **EP2**

February brings relentless gray skies and steady rainfall, the kind of weather that makes you appreciate the solid indoor gathering places scattered around town. Back in the early twentieth century, Covington was driven by five major logging and shingle operations, including the Bereiter Mill at Berrydale and the C.C. Mill on Thomas Road. The thick timber that once fed those saws has mostly given way to residential neighborhoods, but the blue-collar resilience of those early days still lingers in the community's practical spirit.

When the rain turns heavy, head over to Seree Thai Restaurant and Bar on Kent-Kangley Road for a steaming bowl of curry that instantly cuts through the winter damp. Afterwards, duck into the Covington Aquatic Center on SE 272nd Street for an afternoon public swim session under the warm indoor lights. Slipping into the heated water while cold rain taps on the roof overhead is one of the best local tricks for beating the midwinter blues.

Daily logistics in February can be tricky when school traffic mixes with heavy highway congestion along State Route 516. If you are trying to head west toward Kent without getting trapped in the bottleneck near State Route 18, slip onto SE 256th Street instead. This northern bypass keeps your wheels moving and avoids the endless brake lights outside major shopping centers. Having a solid back-road strategy is essential when you live in a town where walking to grab a loaf of bread simply is not realistic.

The international food strip along the Kent-Kangley corridor adds welcome flavor to these gloomy late-winter afternoons. You can see the community's shifting identity in the mix of long-time locals and newer families lining up for hot meals together. Despite the dripping skies, there is a comfortable rhythm to life here as neighbors bump into each other at civic spots like the Covington Public Library. It is a season for staying warm, eating well, and keeping things simple.

### **EP3**

March arrives with sudden sun squalls breaking through passing rain clouds, hinting that the long Pacific Northwest winter is finally easing up. Local lore is full of colorful stories from transitions past, like the historic Berrydale School site that transformed into a prohibition-era cheese factory and later a dance hall rumored to hide secret stills in the surrounding woods. Though that dance hall burned down decades ago, the land around Wax Road still holds a sense of history beneath its modern surface.

Take advantage of a dry afternoon window by picking up fresh sourdough and sweet pastries at Tasty Doux on SE 256th Street. From there, take a short drive to the Soos Creek trail corridor for a refreshing walk along the winding path as early green shoots emerge from the damp soil. Watching the afternoon light flicker through the deciduous trees as the trail runs alongside flowing water makes you forget how close you are to the suburban retail core.

With spring home projects kicking off, the retail centers near Home Depot and Walmart along State Route 516 see heavy weekend traffic. Parking lots fill up quickly around noon, so timing your errands early in the morning is the best way to dodge the rush. Since the town layout requires driving to move between commercial clusters, grouping your errands into one efficient trip saves you from fighting endless red lights.

The changing light in late March brings extra energy to local parks, where dog walkers and runners reclaim the trails during golden hour. You will catch glimpses of the Cascade foothill tree canopies standing sharp against the freshly washed sky. The damp earth smells alive again, signaling that outdoor gathering season is right around the corner. It is a transitional month where brief patches of blue sky are all the excuse anyone needs to step outside.

### **EP4**

Incorporated in August 1997 to give residents direct control over suburban growth, Covington has worked to balance commercial convenience with protected green spaces. That civic effort is especially obvious this time of year, when community parks come alive with activity under clear spring skies.

Grab a sweet morning pick-me-up at Pink Sugar Espresso on 104th Street before heading over to Covington Community Park on SE 240th Street. Walk out onto the elevated wetland boardwalk to take in open views of the surrounding foothill canopy and listen for red-winged blackbirds calling from the reeds. The flat, wide trails here make for an easy spring stroll, offering a peaceful vantage point over the restored natural habitat.

Commuting through the main artery along Kent-Kangley Road can test your nerves during peak morning hours as regional traffic moves toward major freeways. Using Wax Road or cutting through 180th Avenue SE offers a far smoother ride when you are trying to reach residential pockets on the north side. Parking at local parks remains manageable in spring, though hitting the playground before mid-morning helps you land a spot right near the trailhead.

The Coast Salish heritage of this land feels close in spring, as native flora like camas and nettles push up along stream banks much like they did when ancestors of the Muckleshoot Indian Tribe harvested them generations ago. Community spaces like the Covington Public Library host seasonal programming that draws families together on breezy afternoons. With the days growing longer, the town sheds its winter coat and shifts toward open-air living.

### **EP6**

June arrives with maximum solstice energy, bringing clear skies, warm temperatures, and nearly sixteen hours of daylight to Southeast King County. Early logging camps like Meredith Mill and Calhoun Lumber Company once operated under these same sky-high summer suns, clearing thick timber to build regional homes. Modern life here is far less strenuous, but the community's deep connection to outdoor living remains as strong as ever during these peak daylight weeks.

Start an ideal June outing by stopping at CREAM Soft Serve Bar on Kent-Kangley Road for handcrafted ice cream. Take your cold treat across town to Jenkins Creek Park for an afternoon picnic on the open lawn spaces followed by a quiet walk beneath the tall native cedars. The thick tree canopy keeps the creek-side trails surprisingly cool even when afternoon temperatures start to climb into the upper seventies.

Summer road trips and regional travel mean State Route 516 bogs down heavily where it meets State Route 18. Smart locals bypass the main retail bottleneck by taking SE 256th Street westward toward Kent when heading out on weekend adventures. Since running basic errands means jumping between separate shopping centers along the main strip, combining your stops into a single morning outing keeps you out of afternoon bumper-to-bumper traffic.

The neighborhood parks fill with families enjoying early summer sports, while public trails see a steady stream of walkers from sunrise until late evening. The sense of place is rooted in both modern community amenities and ancestral Whulshootseed heritage along local waterways. June sets a relaxed, sunny tone that encourages everyone to slow down and savor the long, warm evenings.

Afterward, take a leisurely stroll around Pipe Lake or head over to the elevated wetland boardwalk at Covington Community Park to catch saltwater-like breezes filtering through the foothill trees. The combination of festival energy and scenic park paths makes for a memorable summer outing.

Navigating town during festival weekend requires extra planning as street closures along 169th Place SE redirect local traffic onto secondary roads. Free surface parking at major retail plazas remains abundant, but finding a spot near Covington Community Park requires arriving before ten in the morning on sunny weekends. The summer atmosphere reaches its height as neighbors catch up along parade routes and children play near park splash pads. Warm July nights invite lingering conversations on front porches as cricket songs fill the cooling evening air.
