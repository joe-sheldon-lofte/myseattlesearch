### **EP1**

Federal Way was built on movement and mid-century modern design. Before officially incorporating as a city in 1990, this stretch of land halfway between Seattle and Tacoma developed around logging routes, David "Papa" Stone's landing at Redondo, and five local school districts that consolidated around the newly finished Federal Highway 99 in 1929. The Muckleshoot and Puyallup tribes lived on these lands for generations before that, navigating Hylebos Creek and Puget Sound shorelines. Today, January wraps these forested ravines and suburban subdivisions in a quiet layer of low morning fog.

When the post-holiday lull sets in, set aside two hours for a calm winter walk. Stop by Driphouse Coffee on SW 356th St for a hot espresso, then make the short drive to West Hylebos Wetlands Park. Stepping onto the continuous, flat wooden boardwalk leads you straight through dense stands of giant Douglas firs and rare peat bogs. The loop takes you past the historic Barker Cabin while damp cedar scents fill the air, creating a serene and accessible trail right in the heart of town.

Getting around Federal Way in winter requires an understanding of its wide street grids. The town's sprawling layout along Pacific Highway South and S 320th St means your car is essential for daily routines, making multi-stop errands on foot nearly impossible. Consolidate your shopping stops into single plazas like The Commons at Federal Way or the H Mart complex to save time. If evening traffic on northbound Interstate 5 backs up near Exit 142, jump off early at S 348th St or use Highway 99 as a direct arterial workaround.

As early darkness arrives before 5:00 PM, the community gravitates toward the warm lights of Koreatown along Pacific Highway South. Stopping into authentic tofu houses or ordering steaming bowls of pho near Paldo World provides a welcome refuge from the cold rain. You can also pick up sweet treats from La Costa Del Sol to round out the evening. The local atmosphere in mid-winter is authentic and grounded, held together by good food and cozy neighborhood routines.

### **EP2**

The architectural identity of Federal Way was permanently altered in 1971 with the completion of the Weyerhaeuser Corporate Headquarters campus. Designed by Edward Charles Bassett and Peter Walker, the iconic five-story glass-and-concrete building was the West Coast's first suburban corporate office park, famously damming a forested ravine. That history of adapting the landscape carries through to modern times as the site evolves into a multi-tenant workspace surrounded by world-class botanical collections. Notable local figures like NFL star Corey Dillon and MLB pitcher Tony Barnette grew up playing on local fields shaped by this rapid suburban growth.

When grey February skies linger, treat yourself to a simple two-hour morning routine. Pick up a warm drink at Good Vibes Espresso before taking a drive over to the Federal Way Community Center on S 333rd St. Spending an hour in the heated indoor lap and leisure pools or relaxing near the steam rooms offers an ideal antidote to damp weather. It is a zero-stress local ritual that lets you stay active while avoiding the winter drizzle outside.

Driving along major arterials like S 320th St during wet winter afternoons requires patience. Parking lots near main storefronts can become crowded quickly with shoppers trying to park as close to building doors as possible. Skip the frustration by parking slightly further out in large shared plazas and walking under covered walkways. For specialty groceries or fresh ingredients, heading straight to the H Mart shopping center eliminates the need to cross busy intersections multiple times.

February is prime season for exploring local comfort food spots across town. Gathering for a meal at Todo Asado Mexican Grill or ordering fresh bakery items from Bon Appetina's brings warmth to a dark afternoon. The quiet tempo of late winter gives residents time to enjoy their favorite local haunts without long waits. Inside these local spots, you will find a welcoming community spirit that keeps South King County warm through the gloom.

### **EP3**

March brings shifting weather patterns that mirror the town's history along the shoreline. At Redondo Beach, David "Papa" Stone established a logging settlement at Stone's Landing in the late 1800s, which eventually grew into a public boardwalk and fishing pier. Coast Salish peoples, including the Muckleshoot and Puyallup tribes, originally used these marine beaches for seasonal fishing and hunting long before timber mills arrived. Today, the shoreline offers a front-row seat to early spring sun squalls rolling across the saltwater.

Take advantage of a sudden spring sunburst with a two-hour coastal outing. Grab a fresh treat from Pinckney Cookie Cafe or a coffee from JSE Coffee near Twin Lakes, then drive down to the Redondo Beach boardwalk. Spend an hour walking the wooden pier, watching scuba divers plunge into Puget Sound, and taking in unobstructed views across to Maury Island and the Olympic Mountains. The salty sea breeze provides an energizing contrast to weeks of indoor living.

As daylight begins to stretch, traffic around major intersections like Pacific Highway South and Military Road S picks up in the late afternoon. Low spring sun glare can slow down drivers on Interstate 5, making local side streets and arterials a smarter choice for commuting. When planning grocery runs or errand loops, stick to established retail clusters along 1st Ave S or S 320th St to avoid making difficult left turns across multi-lane roads.

The atmosphere in March is full of anticipation as green shoots appear across local parks. You can head up to Dumas Bay Centre Park to watch dark storm clouds trade places with bright blue sky over the coastal bluffs. Afterwards, grab lunch at Picanteria el Cevichito for fresh seafood and Mexican flavors. The combination of dramatic coastal weather and reliable neighborhood food spots makes early spring an exciting time to explore town.
