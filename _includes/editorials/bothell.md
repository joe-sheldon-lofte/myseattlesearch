### **EP1**

First settled in 1870 by Columbus S. Greenleaf and George R. Wilson, the area along the lower river was originally known as Squak Slough before logging pioneer George Brackett bought up parcels in 1877. By 1884, David Bothell purchased local timberland to build a home and shingle mill, prompting postmaster Gerhard Ericksen to officially name the post office Bothell in 1888. That early logging spirit still lingers in the bones of the city, anchoring a community that has spent over a century balancing working waterfronts with steady residential growth.

For a classic two-hour winter outing, start on Main Street in downtown Bothell to grab a warm coffee at a local cafe. Walk down toward the Park at Bothell Landing, cross the wooden timber bridge over the quiet Sammamish River, and follow the flat 1.5-mile loop along the Sammamish River Trail toward Blyth Park. The cold air keeps the trail peaceful, offering wide open river views framed by bare winter trees.

Downtown along Main Street and Bothell Way NE allows you to handle most daily errands on foot, but the moment you head north toward Canyon Park or along the Bothell-Everett Highway, you will definitely want your car keys. Straddling the boundary between King County and Snohomish County creates a unique split in municipal planning and service districts that locals learn to navigate early on. If you are parking downtown during busy morning hours, skip searching for street spots and pull directly into the free underground parking garage at Bothell City Hall on levels P2 and P3.

Long before timber mills lined the slough, the Lushootseed-speaking Sammamish people inhabited winter longhouse villages like *ƛ̕ax̌ʷadis* near the mouth of the river. January brings a quiet reset to these ancestral grounds, with low fog hanging soft over the protected shoreline of the former Wayne Golf Course parkland. On raw winter days, you can escape the chilly dampness by ducking into local shop windows downtown or enjoying a low-stimulus nature walk through the quiet, shaded canopy of North Creek Forest.

### **EP2**

Built in 1931 as the art-deco Bothell Junior High campus, McMenamins Anderson School stands as one of the best architectural salvage stories in the region. The transformed 5.4-acre property now holds a hotel, movie theater, ten-keg brewery, and a heated saltwater soaking pool. It serves as a central social anchor where local history and modern community life meet over warm pub fare and cold local pints.

Spend two hours exploring this corner of town by ordering a cozy meal at the Woodshop Bar inside McMenamins Anderson School. After eating, take a short stroll to the Park at Bothell Landing to view the historic 1893 Beckstrom Cabin and 1905 Hannan House, both preserved and relocated to house the Bothell Historical Museum. It is a simple, satisfying way to connect with local roots on a chilly winter afternoon.

Commuting around the SR 522 and I-405 interchange during peak evening hours can test anyone's patience, especially near the Wayne Curve. Smart locals bypass the bumper-to-bumper gridlock by using Beardslee Boulevard or cutting through Canyon Park via 240th Street SE. If you are stopping for quick errands along 101st Avenue NE or NE 183rd, remember that street parking carries a one to two hour limit on weekdays, though it opens up unmetered after 5 PM and on weekends.

February brings steady rain on tin roofs and low gray skies, making warm indoor sanctuaries essential. Down the Bothell-Everett Highway corridor, family-owned East Asian, South Asian, and Middle Eastern markets and eateries cater to tech workers and students from nearby campuses. This multicultural strip comes alive in winter, offering steaming bowls of soup and hot tea when the Pacific Northwest weather turns damp.
