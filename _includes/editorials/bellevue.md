### **EP1**

William Meydenbauer settled this shoreline back in 1869, followed shortly by postmaster Isaac Bechtel naming the area for its expansive views of Lake Washington. Long before high-rise towers held court over the skyline, this ground was famous for sprawling blueberry fields and strawberry crops. That agricultural calm comes back around every January, when the post-holiday lull drops a blanket of stillness over the city. The glass towers lose their December glitz, replacing it with a cool fog hanging low across the water.

When the January dampness settles into your jacket, take a low-key two-hour breather. Start by grabbing a warm drink at Bellevue Coffeeroasters Spotlight down in the urban center, then wander over to the circular promenade at Bellevue Downtown Park. The 240-foot waterfall reflection pond feels remarkably peaceful on a quiet winter morning. From there, take a slow stroll down NE 2nd Street toward Meydenbauer Bay Park, where you can walk out along the curved viewing pier and watch the mist drift across the dark lake surface.

Living here means mastering the contrast between the high-density grid and daily driving needs. While downtown streets are easy enough to navigate on foot with H Mart and Amazon Go right around the corner, residential life in Factoria or Lake Hills requires a car for almost every routine task. When evening traffic picks up, smart locals avoid Bellevue Way South near the I-90 interchange entirely. Sliding over to 112th Ave SE saves you from getting trapped in the north-south crawl.

January is the month to appreciate the quieter corners of local history. Long before Gabe Newell set up Valve inside the Lincoln Square tower or high schoolers like Ann and Nancy Wilson were shaping the sound of Heart at Sammamish High, this city was defined by simple, quiet rhythms. Today, you can step into the dry comfort of the indoor skybridges at The Bellevue Collection when the rain starts pouring, or escape the weather inside the Bellevue Arts Museum. It is a clean, sharp reset to start the year.

### **EP2**

Back in 1946, Kemper Freeman Sr. opened a quiet sixteen-store shopping strip anchored by a neighborhood grocery store. That modest development eventually grew into Bellevue Square, transforming this former berry farming outpost into a major regional hub. February brings a distinct energy to those same halls, as Lunar New Year celebrations take center stage with lively performance traditions and night markets that honor the diverse cultures making up nearly forty percent of our resident population.

When February skies remain stubbornly gray, combat the damp chill with an easy afternoon routine. Head over to Crossroads Bellevue to browse the international food stalls and soak in the community living room vibe. Afterwards, take a short drive to Kelsey Creek Farm to visit the preserved 1930s dairy barns and peaceful pastures. Walking around the historic timber structures while farm animals graze in the crisp winter air offers an unexpectedly grounding breather just minutes from the city center.

Parking in the city center is surprisingly painless if you know where to go. While many regional downtowns charge exorbitant rates, Bellevue Square, Lincoln Square, and Bellevue Place offer thousands of free covered garage spots without demanding ticket validation. The real trick is navigating the commercial corridors along NE 8th Street when weekend shoppers fill the lanes. If you are running quick errands, taking 116th Ave SE parallel to the main strip keeps your sanity intact.

February calls for indoor sanctuaries and rich local food. You can slip into Always Thai Restaurant for a steaming bowl of noodles or swing by Kitz Cafe for a quick morning pastry and coffee. Beyond the dining options, this month highlights the deep cultural fabric woven across the Bel-Red corridor and Coast Salish territories originally tended by the Sammamish and Snoqualmie peoples. Watching low clouds drift across Lake Washington from a dry window seat reminds you why living here feels so distinct.

### **EP3**

Bellevue has always been a city of reinvention, moving from Coast Salish cedar canoe routes to mid-century agricultural boomtown to tech heavyweight. Even digital pioneers like Larry Sanger lived here during pivotal early developments for Wikipedia, drawn to a city that balances rapid innovation with deep green pockets. March captures that duality perfectly as sudden sun squalls break through gray clouds, lighting up both glass office towers and historic wetland channels.

Take advantage of a bright March afternoon by heading straight to the Bellevue Botanical Garden. Stroll through the 53-acre manicured grounds to spot early witch hazel blooms in the Yao Garden and walk across the suspension bridge over Lost Meadow. Follow the forested connector trail up into Wilburton Hill Park canopy loops to catch the early spring buds opening beneath a soaring canopy of fir and cedar.

March weather changes every twenty minutes, which means local routines shift on a dime. The city core is built on wide, flat ADA-compliant pathways and mandatory pedestrian corridors, making a quick run to QFC or Whole Foods easy on foot. However, if you are driving south toward I-405 during the evening rush hour between four and six-thirty, skip Bellevue Way South entirely. Cutting across 112th Ave SE avoids the bumper-to-bumper standstill.

Down along Mercer Slough Nature Park, more than seven miles of wooden boardwalks weave through quiet, awakening wetlands. This area was once part of the primary fishing and gathering grounds for local Coast Salish tribes, and today it serves as a serene escape from urban noise. As daylight stretches a bit longer each evening, locals begin gathering on the hilltop street-end overlooks in the Somerset neighborhood to watch setting sunbeams bounce off Lake Washington.

### **EP4**

Before Bellevue Arts Museum occupied its striking Steven Holl-designed facility on Bellevue Way, it started humbly in 1947 as a community craft fair on the top deck of the Bellevue Square parking garage. That scrappy, DIY transformation mirrors the city itself. By April, as cherry blossoms bloom across the urban grid, you can see how thoughtfully planned open plaza setbacks and public spaces have integrated art into everyday life.

Spend a mild April morning exploring the historic heart of Old Bellevue along Main Street. Grab a fresh cup from Alaskan or Bellevue Coffeeroasters Spotlight, then take a leisurely stroll down the brick-lined sidewalks. Walk two blocks north into Bellevue Downtown Park to loop the circular promenade beneath blossoming cherry trees, catching the morning light reflecting off the central pond before heading back for a quiet patio break.

As daylight stretches past seven-thirty in the evening, neighborhood traffic patterns start shifting. Finding street parking in Old Bellevue can get tight during prime lunch hours, but downtown meters offer a handy two-hour free window enforced Monday through Saturday. If those spots are full, remember that Bellevue Downtown Park maintains a dedicated free civic lot off 100th Ave NE that usually has openings early in the day.

April brings a renewed energy to the local food truck scene and neighborhood spots. You can grab quick, authentic bites from Taco Alebrije or Taqueria El Cerrito for an easy outdoor lunch. Whether you are strolling past the flowering trees near City Hall or listening to the rain tap against cafe windows, spring in Bellevue offers a crisp, optimistic atmosphere that makes everyone want to grab a light jacket and head outside.

### **EP5**

Long before MLB Cy Young winner Tim Lincecum was pitching at Liberty High School, Bellevue was building a reputation for fostering young talent across athletics and the arts. That spirit continues every May with spring productions at Bellevue Youth Theatre and seasonal community gatherings. The city's investment in public recreation keeps its agricultural heritage alive, giving modern residents a direct link to the past right in the middle of a tech-driven landscape.

May is prime time for a family trip to Kelsey Creek Farm. Stroll past the historic timber barns to greet the spring baby animals out in the green pastures, then follow the quiet nature trails winding through the forest perimeter. After visiting the farm, take a quick ten-minute drive down to Chism Beach Park to sit on the bluff picnic benches, enjoying expansive views over Lake Washington toward Mercer Island as the afternoon sun lingers.

Navigating the city in late spring requires knowing how to dodge arterial bottlenecks. While Coal Creek Parkway and NE 8th Street handle heavy suburban commute traffic for neighborhoods like Lake Hills, switching to parallel residential routes makes running weekend errands far smoother. If you are taking public transit, the Sound Transit 2 Line Light Rail gives seamless regional connectivity between Bellevue and Redmond, helping you skip highway delays altogether.

Long twilights define May in the Pacific Northwest, with daylight extending well into the evening. Grab a hearty sandwich or fish lunch at Salty Blue Fish & Chips, or sample authentic Mexican flavors from El Saborcito or Taqueria Jalisco. As dusk settles, locals head to public spots like Meydenbauer Bay Park to watch the setting sun paint the water in shades of amber and rose.

### **EP6**

When postmaster Isaac Bechtel officially named Bellevue in 1882 for its breathtaking views, June was undoubtedly the month he had in mind. Early settlers logged dense stands of Douglas fir and cleared land for berry farms, creating a unique relationship between human enterprise and natural beauty. Today, that connection persists along the waterfront, where sleek public parks frame the same sweeping lake vistas that inspired the city name over a century ago.

Capitalize on solstice light with a two-hour waterfront outing. Start in Old Bellevue with a sweet treat from PJ's Classic Creamery, then walk west along NE 2nd Street to Meydenbauer Bay Park. Use the public street-to-beach elevator to glide past the steep hillside stairways down to the shore. Take a long walk out onto the curved viewing pier, watching paddleboarders navigate the calm waters of Lake Washington under the June sun.

June weekends bring large crowds to local waterfronts, so timing your outing is everything. The terraced beach at Meydenbauer Bay Park often fills up by late morning, making early morning or post-seven evening visits ideal for securing quiet pier seating. If you are driving downtown, skip searching for paid street spots and park in the massive covered garages at Bellevue Place or Lincoln Square, where parking remains completely free for visitors.

June signals the launch of spirited summer routines, including the Bellevue Farmers Market opening days and outdoor terrace dining across the city. Stop by Taqueria Entrada & Salida or Little Sonora for flavorful street-style tacos before hitting the parks. As the sun lingers past nine PM, neighbors linger in public plazas, enjoying warm evening air and the effortless blend of urban living and natural shoreline.

### **EP7**

In July 1947, a small group of local creators hung artwork on the top railing of a parking garage, giving birth to what would become the Bellevue Arts Fair Weekend. Decades later, that event draws thousands of visitors every July to celebrate northwest craftsmanship. The city's agricultural legacy also shines bright this month, as public blueberry picking opens for free at Bellevue Blueberry Park and Mercer Slough, continuing a tradition rooted in the town's original soil.

Plan an unforgettable July afternoon by picking fresh blueberries at Bellevue Blueberry Park. Bring a bucket and spend an hour harvesting ripe fruit right off the bushes under the blue sky. Afterwards, cool off with a quick drive down to Bellevue Downtown Park to sit on the grassy lawn near the waterfall promenade, catching one of the free summer outdoor concert performances that take place on summer afternoons and evenings.

During Bellevue Arts Fair Weekend in late July, traffic around Bellevue Way and NE 8th Street comes to a crawl. Smart locals leave their cars behind or use alternative north-south routes like 112th Ave SE. If you are grabbing groceries or running daily errands downtown during fair weekend, foot traffic is high, but walkable access to shops like Amazon Go and H Mart makes skipping the parking garage stress simple.

Peak summer calls for fresh seafood and cold drinks on outdoor patios. You can visit Shark Bite Ceviches for citrus-cured seafood or grab a pint at Bellevue Beer Gardens Spotlight down in the downtown core. For beer enthusiasts willing to venture slightly north, Ladd & Lass Brewing or Bickersons Brewhouse offer fantastic seasonal pints. End your summer evening watching ferry boats cross Lake Washington in the distance as saltwater breezes drift inland.

### **EP8**

Bellevue has always attracted original thinkers, from Tony-nominated Broadway star Megan Hilty who grew up attending Sammamish High School to tech innovators shaping modern software. August brings a golden, laid-back haze across the community, where high-energy routines give way to long afternoons by the water. The city’s strict zoning guidelines keep high-density towers tucked neatly into the central core, leaving quiet residential neighborhoods surrounded by mature trees and clear lake access.

Escape late August heat with a two-hour water retreat. Head down to Enatai Beach Park or Chism Beach Park for a refreshing afternoon swim in Lake Washington. Afterwards, take a short drive over to Newcastle Golf Club and step onto the hilltop patio overlook. Sitting high above the city with a cold beverage while watching the late afternoon sun cast a golden glow across the water and Seattle skyline is one of the best summer rituals in the region.

When August heat spikes, navigating local parking requires a bit of tactical planning. Surface lots at lakefront parks hit capacity early on weekends, but you can always rely on the cool, subterranean parking structures beneath Bellevue Square and Lincoln Square for effortless, shaded parking. Daily errands are easiest in the early morning before arterial roads like Coal Creek Parkway get backed up with beachgoers.

Late summer evenings belong on outdoor patios along Bellevue Way and downtown corridors. Gather with friends for local brews at Machine House Brewery or Hill City Tap House & Bottle Shop, or sample coastal flavors from Baiana and Alaska Weathervane Scallop. As golden hour fades into twilight, warm breezes carry the scent of sweet pine and lake water, creating an unforgettable late-summer atmosphere across the Eastside.

### **EP9**

Long before European settlers logged the forests, the waterways of Mercer Slough were vital seasonal gathering grounds and cedar canoe transit routes for the Sammamish and Snoqualmie peoples. In September, as back-to-school energy settles over the city, these ancient wetland channels return to a peaceful rhythm. The natural history of the slough serves as a quiet reminder of the deep indigenous heritage underpinning this modern landscape.

Spend a crisp September morning exploring the Mercer Slough canoe trails. Launch a kayak or canoe into the quiet waterways, paddling under shaded tree canopies where early autumn colors are just starting to show. Follow up your paddle with a visit to Marcenet Mercantile or Seattle Poi Company for a special artisanal treat, enjoying the quiet morning calm before the afternoon hustle begins.

September brings school buses and morning traffic back to major arterials like NE 8th Street and Bellevue Way. If you are commuting across town between eight and nine in the morning, taking secondary connectors like 116th Ave SE helps you bypass back-ups near school zones. For errands, downtown remains exceptionally walkable, allowing you to grab groceries at QFC or Whole Foods without fighting school rush hours.

The air turns noticeably sharper by mid-September, bringing clear views of distant mountain peaks. You can wander along the Coal Creek Natural Area stream trail to listen to the rushing water and watch early yellow leaves drift onto the damp path. It is a transitional month filled with golden light, crisp air, and a welcome return to cozy community routines.

### **EP10**

As autumn rain begins to mist the Eastside, Bellevue’s cultural calendar moves indoors. The annual Bellevue Jazz & Blues Series brings top-tier live music to venues across the city, celebrating creative expression in a community built on global connections. With nearly forty percent of residents bringing international perspectives to local neighborhoods, October highlights how diverse cultural influences enrich everything from public performance to daily culinary life.

Embrace a damp October afternoon with a fall foliage stroll through Wilburton Hill Park. Follow the wide dirt paths beneath towering maples turning brilliant shades of orange and yellow, then connect directly to the upper perimeter paths of the Bellevue Botanical Garden. Afterwards, head into Old Bellevue for a bowl of piping hot soup or ramen from Always Thai Restaurant or a warm beverage at Kitz Cafe to take off the autumn chill.

October weather can turn from brisk sun to steady downpours in minutes. When outdoor walks get rained out, smart locals pivot to indoor routes like the climate-controlled pedestrian skybridges linking The Bellevue Collection towers. Parking in covered structures like Bellevue Place keeps you entirely dry while running errands, avoiding wet street parking meters altogether.

There is a unique beauty to damp autumn evenings in the Pacific Northwest. Rain slicks the wide downtown sidewalks while glowing windows invite you inside cozy dining spots across the Bel-Red corridor and Crossroads Bellevue. Whether you are listening to live saxophones at a jazz venue or enjoying quiet sensory moments along a misty forest trail, October offers a deeply satisfying seasonal rhythm.

### **EP11**

November brings the first real winter storms off Puget Sound and Lake Washington, drumming against roofs and windowpanes across the city. This season highlights the foresight of Bellevue’s urban design, which mandates generous plaza setbacks, covered walkways, and subterranean parking to keep daily life moving smoothly regardless of weather. It is a month where the contrast between rugged Pacific Northwest wilderness and polished urban infrastructure feels sharpest.

Take advantage of a stormy November afternoon by storm-watching from Meydenbauer Bay Park. Watch whitecaps roll across Lake Washington from the protected viewing pier, then warm up with a fresh roast from Bellevue Coffeeroasters Spotlight. If you prefer to stay completely dry, spend an hour browsing contemporary exhibits inside the Steven Holl-designed Bellevue Arts Museum before slipping into a cozy booth nearby.

The Friday after Thanksgiving marks the grand launch of Snowflake Lane (running November 27 through December 24), which brings nightly holiday parades along Bellevue Way. Traffic around the city core becomes heavy starting around five PM during event nights. To avoid gridlock, use 112th Ave SE or 116th Ave SE when passing through the area, or park early in the free Bellevue Square garages before the evening parade crowds arrive.

November also sees the annual opening of the Bellevue Downtown Ice Rink, bringing festive outdoor skating to Bellevue Downtown Park. After a session on the ice, swing by local food trucks like Taco Alebrije or grab hot take-out from Salty Blue Fish & Chips. As early darkness falls around four-thirty PM, the city glows with warm streetlights and early holiday decorations, offering a cozy sanctuary from the cold rain outside.

### **EP12**

December in Bellevue is a full-spectrum holiday experience anchored by long-standing traditions. What started decades ago as modest community shopping displays has evolved into major regional events like Snowflake Lane along Bellevue Way and Garden d'Lights at Bellevue Botanical Garden. Despite the city's growth into a major tech metropolis housing companies like Microsoft, Amazon, Meta, and Valve, the December atmosphere retains a strong sense of civic connection and holiday warmth.

Bundle up for a magical December evening outing. Start at the Bellevue Botanical Garden to walk through Garden d'Lights, where thousands of miniature lights illuminate plant-shaped sculptures along quiet woodland paths. Afterwards, head downtown to line the sidewalks of Bellevue Way for the nightly Snowflake Lane parade, watching falling artificial snow, toy soldiers, and festive floats light up the street beneath the glowing high-rise towers.

Holiday shopping across The Bellevue Collection draws visitors from across the Pacific Northwest, making parking strategy essential. While the main garages at Bellevue Square fill quickly on December weekends, opting for lower levels at Lincoln Square or Bellevue Place often yields stress-free parking spots with easy skybridge access. If you need to pick up quick groceries, neighborhood spots like H Mart or Uwajimaya offer fantastic options without navigating mall traffic.

End your December evening with a warm drink from Alaskan or a scoop from PJ's Classic Creamery. You can also explore the diverse international offerings at the Crossroads Bellevue food court or sample local craft brews at Ladd & Lass Brewing. As the year draws to a close, twinkling lights line the circular promenade at Bellevue Downtown Park, reflecting off the dark water and creating a peaceful winter scene in the heart of the city.
