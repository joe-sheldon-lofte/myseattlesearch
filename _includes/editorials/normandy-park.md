### **EP1**

January drops a quiet, foggy curtain across the Puget Sound bluffs, wrapping the residential canopy in a slow winter reset. Long before modern cul-de-sacs took root, the Seattle-Tacoma Land Company platted this land in 1929 with a distinct French Normandy vision. You can still see that original architectural benchmark at C.S. Hughett’s 1929 brick home along Normandy Terrace SW, setting a stately, woodsy tone that survived decades of suburban growth.

On a chilly morning, head over to Cafe Dulzura on Des Moines Memorial Dr S for a piping hot espresso and a fresh pastry. Take your warm cup down to Marine View Park at 20900 Marine View Dr SW, where you can descend the paved access road and the three-story wooden staircase through coastal forest. The quiet 0.2-mile gravel shore at the bottom offers uninterrupted views across Puget Sound, perfect for a peaceful winter stroll.

Living here requires a bit of daily navigational strategy because Normandy Park keeps a strictly low-density residential footprint. You will not find a massive commercial grocery grid inside city limits, so fetching milk or dinner supplies means driving five minutes into downtown Burien or slipping out to 1st Avenue South. During morning peak hours, savvy locals bypass 1st Avenue South congestion by cutting down 4th Avenue SW or SW Normandy Road instead.

The atmosphere this month is pure coastal sanctuary. While the post-holiday rush fades, locals take quiet walks along the flat, paved perimeter loop at Marvista Park. These lands carry deep Coast Salish roots as traditional gathering places for the Duwamish, Muckleshoot, and Suquamish tribes, and standing near Miller and Walker Creeks in January reminds you how deeply nature still governs this hillside enclave.

### **EP2**

February brings low gray clouds and steady drizzles, the kind of weather that makes you appreciate the civic stubbornness of this community. Back in June of 1953, local residents officially voted to incorporate as the City of Normandy Park specifically to protect their local zoning, save the dense tree canopy, and maintain independent municipal control. That decision kept the grand old firs standing, creating the quiet neighborhood lanes we drive today.

When winter lulls call for comfort, pick up a box of fresh artisan cookies from Lowrider Cookie Company on SW 153rd St. Drive over to Marvista Park on 4th Ave SW and head for the covered gazebo. It is a simple, unscripted joy to sit under cover with a sweet treat while watching storm clouds roll past the open lawns and Puget Sound beyond.

Finding parking at Marine View Park is surprisingly easy in February compared to summer, as the fifteen to twenty spot lot rarely fills up in the rain. Just keep in mind that Normandy Park sits directly along SeaTac Airport flight paths, so depending on winter wind directions, jet noise can echo across the eastern hills. For quick errands, stick to the 1st Avenue South corridor to grab what you need before retreating back to peaceful side streets.

The season invites cozy indoor stops and gentle walks. When the rain breaks, you might pop into Zain Restaurant And Bakery Cafe for warm pastries or grab a spiced drink at Dubai Cafeteria & Expresso. The town remains a deeply peaceful sanctuary where damp cedar scents fill the air and the saltwater breeze provides a sharp, refreshing reminder of life on the Sound.

### **EP3**

March brings sudden sun squalls and the first subtle teases of Pacific Northwest spring. The shaded forest trails we enjoy today exist largely thanks to early developer and councilmember Ron Walker, who famously championed saving mature trees during subdivision construction. The thirty-acre Walker Preserve was named in his honor, preserving a wild green spine in the middle of town.

Start your morning by grabbing treats from Orange Bakery on SW 153rd St before making your way to Walker Preserve on 2nd Ave SW. A walk along the dirt trails takes you deep into a lush canopy between Walker Creek and Miller Creek, where you can spot early trilliums blooming along the ravine slopes. The gentle murmur of running water makes it feel hours away from city life.

If you venture down to the beach at Marine View Park or trek into Walker Preserve, remember that these coastal ravines are steep. The three-story wooden staircase at Marine View Park demands sturdy footwear, and you should always double your time estimate for the uphill climb back to your car. To dodge regional commuter backups, use SW Normandy Road as your relief route away from highway traffic.

March light shifts rapidly from bright sunshine to sudden rain showers in a matter of minutes. That dynamic weather gives pocket parks like Nature Trails Park a fresh, dramatic feel as the yellow and red trail loops begin to thaw. It is a wonderful time to watch the town wake up, grounded in ancestral Coast Salish territory where freshwater streams feed directly into Puget Sound.

### **EP4**

April arrives with daylight stretching past seven thirty in the evening and cherry blossoms brightening residential driveways. Normandy Park has always been sustained by dedicated residents like Betty Binz, a beloved community pioneer whose decades of service were famously celebrated with a Grown Up Prom at The Cove. That neighborhood spirit extends to the unique co-ownership of Lot A, where over eighteen hundred households hold private deeded rights to the coastal waterfront.

Grab a hot drink at Fivestar Coffee on International Blvd before heading over to Marvista Park. Walk the paved perimeter loop to check out the early community garden plot preparations. As the sun sets late, sitting on the rolling lawn with Puget Sound breezes blowing through the trees is one of the easiest ways to unwind.

Getting around Normandy Park means accepting its non-commercial character. There are no sprawling strip malls within these two point five square miles, so daily logistics always involve a quick car trip over to 1st Avenue South. Keep an eye on local flight corridors overhead, as incoming planes dip low over the southern and eastern ridges on clear spring afternoons.

The atmospheric mood in April is all about spring awakening. Flowers bloom along SW Shorebrook Drive, and the salt air off Puget Sound carries a crisp, clean scent. Standing at a bluff overlook while looking across ancestral waters once fished by Suquamish and Duwamish tribes, you feel the seamless balance between residential quiet and wild marine shoreline.

### **EP5**

May brings long twilights and pre-summer energy that pulls everyone outdoors. The history of Normandy Park's waterfront identity traces back to 1929, when original developers set aside Lot A as an eighteen-acre beach tract along Puget Sound. That deep connection to the water defines how locals spend their warmer months.

Swing by Taqueria Jalisco on S 120th St for a quick taco lunch, then drive down to Marine View Park at 20900 Marine View Dr SW. Spring negative low tides expose wide gravel shores, making it a perfect time to take the wooden stairs down for tidepooling. Searching for tiny shore crabs and marine life along the beach is an unscripted local ritual.

Navigating summer parking requires timing and strict attention to local rules. The lot at Marine View Park holds only about fifteen to twenty vehicles, so arrive before nine in the morning or after five in the evening on warm weekends to guarantee a spot. Remember that The Cove property and lot are strictly private for deeded Lot A owners, and unauthorized cars will be towed quickly.

As May evenings stretch out, Nature Trails Park offers a tranquil, low-stimulus alternative under a shaded evergreen canopy. The scent of sweet cedar fills the air, and barred owl calls echo through the trees. It is a wonderful prelude to summer, blending community warmth with the untamed beauty of the Pacific Northwest coastline.

### **EP6**

June delivers solstice energy, long twilight walks, and the return of favorite neighborhood traditions. Local resident and Seattle Times Art Director Dina Skeels captures this season perfectly through her hand-designed concert posters for the annual Music in the Park summer series at Marvista Park, bringing artistic flair to community gatherings.

Pick up slow-smoked barbecue from the Melton’s BBQ food truck on SW 152nd St, then head over to Marvista Park to enjoy an outdoor dinner on the grass. The flat, paved paths and open fields make it easy to wander while kids play and neighbors catch up under the lingering solstice sun.

With summer travel ramping up, traffic on SR 509 and 1st Avenue South can grind during afternoon hours. Smart commuters rely on 4th Avenue SW to bypass regional bottlenecks. When running daily errands, combining trips into nearby downtown Burien saves you from sitting in unnecessary traffic jams.

June also marks the launch of Food Truck Wednesdays down at The Cove clubhouse, a beloved spot rebuilt in 1956 to replace an earlier 1920s structure. Watching the sun set slowly over Puget Sound while enjoying food truck bites encapsulates the easygoing, maritime lifestyle that makes living in this coastal enclave so special.

### **EP7**

July is peak summer, bringing eighty-degree days, cool saltwater breezes, and proud neighborhood celebrations. The annual Fourth of July Parade along Marine View Drive fills the street with vintage cars, decorated bicycles, and local marching groups, showcasing the tight-knit identity that has defined the town since incorporation.

Order fresh seafood tacos from Mariscos Los Mochis on 6th Ave SW before heading to Marvista Park on a Sunday afternoon. Spread a blanket on the lawn for the five o'clock Music in the Park concert series, where live tunes and ocean breezes create the ultimate summer backdrop.

Peak summer heat means Marine View Park fills up fast. If you want to spend the day tidepooling or relaxing on the beach, plan your arrival around early morning low tides to beat the parking scramble. For grocery shopping, stick to early hours along 1st Avenue South to dodge both summer heat and store crowds.

Down along the shoreline, Puget Sound sparkles under brilliant blue skies while ferries cross the horizon toward the Olympic Mountains. The shaded ravines of Miller Creek and Walker Creek offer a cool refuge from the sun, preserving critical salmon habitat and reminding us of the rich Coast Salish history embedded in this landscape.

### **EP8**

August carries a warm late-summer haze and golden hour light that paints the neighborhood canopy. Normandy Park’s architectural heritage, from French Normandy motifs to mid-century coastal homes, feels especially timeless when bathed in afternoon sunlight.

Grab a bite from Pigfish Cafe on SW 152nd St, then take a drive down to the Marine View Park bluff overlook. Sit on one of the public view benches to take in the vast views of Puget Sound while watching boats glide across the water in the warm August breeze.

Late summer brings shifting wind patterns, which can alter SeaTac flight paths and send plane noise over different parts of town. Keep this in mind when planning outdoor gatherings. To dodge weekend traffic heading to saltwater beaches elsewhere, stay local and use 4th Avenue SW for quick neighborhood travels.

The season reaches a high note with the annual Music in the Park Jazz Festival grand finale at Marvista Park. Gathering on the lawn with friends as golden light fades into twilight offers a perfect farewell to peak summer, grounded in simple community rituals and ocean air.

### **EP9**

September brings a crisp back-to-school atmosphere, warm afternoons, and a subtle shift in the light. The traditional ancestral territories of the Duwamish, Muckleshoot, and Suquamish tribes feel especially vivid as autumn begins to touch the forest, turning the bigleaf maples yellow along the stream banks.

Stop by Cafe Dulzura on Des Moines Memorial Dr S for a handcrafted coffee, then head to Walker Preserve on 2nd Ave SW. Walking the quiet dirt trails alongside Walker Creek lets you experience the early fall colors filtering through the dense evergreen canopy without the summer crowds.

School schedules mean morning and afternoon traffic shifts around local Highline district routes. Plan your daily errands around these peak times, using SW Normandy Road to navigate smoothly between neighborhood pockets and nearby shopping centers.

The weather in September is arguably some of the finest in the Pacific Northwest, featuring clear blue skies and sharp autumn light on the Olympic Mountains. A late afternoon walk through Nature Trails Park provides a serene, sensory retreat as the days begin to shorten.

### **EP10**

October coats the town in deep autumn foliage, damp forest soil, and cozy fall traditions. The annual Normandy Park Autumn Fest at Marvista Park brings families together for seasonal games and celebrations, continuing a long tradition of community gatherings that center around local parks and The Cove clubhouse.

Pick up a warm bowl of birria from Birrieria Mi Tierra on Marine View Drive, then head to the footbridges over Walker Creek. October marks the return of coho and chum salmon, and standing above the stream to watch these fish complete their journey is an unforgettable local ritual.

Fallen leaves make the steep wooden staircase at Marine View Park slick, so watch your footing on wet days. Remember that parking rules at private spots like Lot A are strictly enforced year-round, so stick to public park lots or public trailheads when heading out for fall walks.

Crisp air and woodsmoke drift through the trees along SW Normandy Road as early dusk settles over the neighborhood. The contrast between the roaring Puget Sound surf and the quiet sanctuary of shaded forest trails makes October one of the most evocative months of the year.

### **EP11**

November arrives with the first heavy winter rainstorms, pounding rain on tin roofs and dramatic waves along Puget Sound. The decision by 1953 founders to preserve Normandy Park's two point five square miles as an independent, green oasis feels especially wise when storm season turns the town into a quiet, cozy retreat.

Pick up sweet treats from Lowrider Cookie Company on SW 153rd St, then drive to the Marine View Park parking overlook. Park facing the water and watch the storm roll across Puget Sound from the comfort and warmth of your car while enjoying your fresh cookies.

With darkness falling by four thirty in the afternoon and slippery trail conditions on steep ravine paths, trade muddy forest hikes for paved walks around Marvista Park. If you need groceries or supplies, stick to the commercial strip on 1st Avenue South to complete your errands before dark.

Inside the forest ravines, Miller Creek and Walker Creek swell with rainwater, carrying the final returning salmon upstream. The sound of heavy rain dripping through cedar boughs offers a peaceful, low-stimulus environment that makes November a favored time for quiet introspection.

### **EP12**

December wraps Normandy Park in festive village light displays and holiday warmth. Community advocate Betty Binz helped foster the neighborly spirit that shines brightly during December traditions, including the annual Holiday Bazaar hosted at The Cove clubhouse at the end of SW Shorebrook Drive.

Treat yourself to a warm specialty drink at Dubai Cafeteria & Expresso on Tukwila International Blvd, then take an evening drive or stroll past the festive light displays along SW Shorebrook Drive. The holiday lights reflected against the dark Puget Sound waters create a magical winter scene.

Holiday errands can cause backups on main thoroughfares, so bypass 1st Avenue South bottlenecks by using 4th Avenue SW for local trips. For major shopping runs, taking quick five-minute drives into downtown Burien keeps your holiday logistics simple and stress-free.

December ends the year on a note of cozy community connection. Whether you are catching up with neighbors at local gift stops or watching winter mist float over the bluffs at Marine View Park, Normandy Park remains a cherished coastal haven grounded in rich heritage and natural beauty.
