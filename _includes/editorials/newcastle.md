### **EP1**

January in Newcastle arrives with a quiet fog hanging low over the Coal Creek valley. Long before Seattle Seahawks players and coaching staff moving into the nearby Virginia Mason Athletic Center in Renton built their homes along these steep ridges, this hillside was a rough logging hamlet. That changed overnight in 1869 when coal was discovered along Coal Creek, transforming the slope into an industrial titan that supplied early Seattle and San Francisco with essential fuel.

These days, the daily routine revolves around the compact commercial core at Coal Creek Parkway and Newcastle Way. Getting through here on a damp January weekday means competing with commuter traffic heading toward I-405, so locals know to slip down 116th Ave SE or SE May Valley Road to bypass the worst gridlock. The town center is handy with the QFC grocery store, but the moment you leave the immediate strip, you will definitely want your car keys to tackle the steep neighborhood inclines.

For a peaceful winter morning, order a warm drink from Ginkgo Coffee on 119th Ave SE. Take your cup and drive up Newcastle Golf Club Road to the public grounds at The Golf Club at Newcastle. Walking along the hilltop paths gives you sweeping zero-cost views of Lake Washington and the Seattle skyline shrouded in winter mist, looking across land that used to be the heavy surface mining grounds of Boren Pit.

End the cold loop down at Lake Boren Park, where the paved loops offer an easy, flat walk along the shoreline. Snoqualmie, Duwamish, and Muckleshoot ancestors fished and gathered along these watersheds for generations before industrial rails ever cut through the trees. Today, watching waterfowl drift past the quiet fishing pier under low grey clouds is about as soothing a post-holiday reset as you can get.

### **EP2**

February brings steady grey drizzle on the roof and that mid-winter longing for warmth. It is hard to imagine while driving past sleek modern homes, but early Newcastle was once Seattle's hardscrabble rival, producing over eleven million tons of coal before the last mines closed in 1963. The historic Seattle and Walla Walla Railroad was built in the 1870s just to haul that black ore down to Elliott Bay piers, essentially funding the economic engine of the whole region.

Dealing with winter errands along Coal Creek Parkway SE requires a bit of strategy when wet weather hits. Parking spots right in front of the local shops fill quickly, but the short walk between the grocery plaza and nearby spots is manageable if you keep a rain jacket handy. When the rain turns heavy, taking refuge inside the Newcastle Library gives you a dry sanctuary to browse regional history records or catch up on reading without spending a dime.

A solid two-hour remedy for a dark February evening begins at Resonate Brewery + Pizzeria on 119th Ave SE. Grab a booth, order a hot craft pizza slice along with a house-brewed beer, and enjoy the cozy local chatter. Afterwards, button up your coat and make the short ten-minute walk down to Lake Boren Park to catch the quiet dusk light reflecting off the water.

If you wander up toward the forest edges near the old mining cuts, you are walking near history that shaped the town's very identity. When residents voted to incorporate back in September 1994, they originally chose the name Newport Hills, but quickly shifted to Newcastle before final incorporation to avoid postal confusion with Bellevue and to honor the pioneer miners who worked these hills. That deep coal heritage keeps this hilltop community grounded even through the darkest winter lulls.

### **EP3**

March in Newcastle is a spectacle of sudden sun squalls breaking through dark clouds over the ridge. You can stand on the high viewpoints and watch weather systems roll across Lake Washington toward the Olympic Mountains. Tucked away in these hills is the historic Newcastle Cemetery, established in the late 1800s, where pioneer coal miners from Wales, Italy, China, and across the globe rest today, silent reminders of the international workforce that built this rugged territory.

Kick off a brisk early spring morning by grabbing a beverage from Newcastle Coffeeroasters Spotlight or Starbucks in the main commercial core. Take your cup down to Lake Boren Park, where ADA-compliant paved pathways make for an effortless stroll around the water regardless of spring mud. Watching the early light hit the wooden boardwalk and fishing pier is a great way to shake off winter sluggishness.

As spring activities pick up, afternoon traffic near the I-405 interchange on Coal Creek Parkway SE begins to back up earlier in the day. Smart drivers head down SE May Valley Road to cut across toward Renton or use 116th Ave SE to navigate toward East Bellevue without getting stuck in bumper-to-bumper lines. Having those backroom shortcuts mapped out saves immense frustration during daily errands.

Later in the afternoon, lace up your boots for a free walk along the Coal Creek Regional Park trails. These forest paths follow ancient rail beds and pass historic coal mine openings hidden beneath emerging green fern fronds. The Snoqualmie and Duwamish people used these same stream corridors for plant gathering and hunting long before industrial tracks were laid, making every footbridge crossing a step through layers of local history.

### **EP4**

April brings an unmistakable shift as daylight stretches past 7:30 PM and fresh green growth bursts through the forest canopy. The residential streets on the upper hillsides come alive with early spring blooms, framing views of the skyline across the water. Among the notable figures who made these tranquil heights their home was Alan White, the legendary drummer for the rock band Yes and Rock and Roll Hall of Fame inductee, who spent many years living right here in Newcastle.

Spring light also means it is time to map out your errands around town. The central shopping plaza anchored by QFC and local spots like Cloud 9 Burgers is compact enough to handle multiple errands in one quick stop. Just remember that while downtown is friendly for walking between storefronts, navigating the steep residential hills surrounding the Bowl requires a dependable vehicle.

For an easy spring afternoon outing, grab a box of fresh treats from Pinckney Cookie Cafe or QFC Bakery, then head straight up to the public view terrace at The Golf Club at Newcastle on Six Penny Lane. You do not need to buy a round of golf to enjoy the panorama, as the sweeping overlook offers free views of Downtown Seattle, Mercer Island, and the blue waters of Lake Washington.

Finish your day with a peaceful loop along the Coal Creek Natural Area trailheads. The upper reaches of the forest canopy offer a sensory-quiet retreat where the rush of stream water covers up any distant city noise. It is an ideal spot to catch early evening light filtering through the trees before heading back down into town.

### **EP5**

May brings pre-summer energy and long, soft twilights that invite you to stay outdoors well after dinner. The foothill breezes blowing across the ridges carry a faint hint of saltwater from Puget Sound. It is fascinating to realize that the grand green slopes of The Golf Club at Newcastle were actually developed directly atop former heavy surface coal mining operations known as Boren Pit, turning an industrial extraction site into 350 acres of rolling green space.

Start a May evening with dinner at Yea's Wok on Coal Creek Parkway SE, a long-standing local favorite for Chinese cuisine that anchors the town's growing international food scene. Afterwards, take a five-minute drive down to Lake Boren Park for an evening walk along the paved lakeside loop while the sun sinks toward the horizon.

Late spring weather brings out strollers, joggers, and dog walkers across every neighborhood trail. Parking at Lake Boren Park's main lot starts to fill up faster on warm evenings, so locals head for the secondary waterline parking area off SE 84th Way to avoid circling for a spot. Keeping this park access secret in your back pocket makes spontaneous evening outings completely stress free.

As dusk settles, people gather around the open grassy lawns and covered picnic pavilions near the lake. The park serves as an inclusive community space where families and neighbors of all backgrounds mix naturally. Watching kids play on the lawns and locals hitting balls on the pickleball courts captures the simple, unpretentious spirit of modern Newcastle.

### **EP6**

Solstice season in Newcastle means daylight lingering past 9:00 PM, painting the sky in deep shades of gold and violet. This land was navigated for centuries by the Snoqualmie, Duwamish, and Muckleshoot tribes, who utilized the Coal Creek and May Creek watersheds for seasonal hunting and fishing. Today, that rich natural landscape forms a dramatic backdrop for solstice gatherings and summer park events.

Midsummer traffic on Coal Creek Parkway SE can get sluggish during the late afternoon rush, especially with people heading toward Lake Boren Park for evening outdoor gatherings. If you are trying to cut across town without getting caught in bumper-to-bumper queues, taking 116th Ave SE or shifting south toward SE May Valley Road remains the absolute best way to keep moving.

Treat yourself to an unscripted June outing by grabbing authentic street tacos from Isidros Food Truck or Taqueria Los Tetillas, then driving up Six Penny Lane to the hilltop grounds at Newcastle Golf Club. Find an open spot on the public viewing grounds to eat while enjoying 180-degree panoramic views of Lake Washington and the distant skyline as the sun sets behind the Olympic peaks.

Cap off the long evening down at the Lake Boren Park stage, where local summer concert series and community gatherings begin to take off. The open lawns fill with picnic blankets and lawn chairs as neighbors greet each other in the warm air. It is peer-to-peer neighborhood life at its best, completely free of corporate gloss or manufactured hype.

### **EP7**

July delivers peak Pacific Northwest summer, with temperatures hovering in the warm eighties and a reliable saltwater breeze blowing off Puget Sound. Newcastle's proximity to the Seahawks training facilities at the Virginia Mason Athletic Center in Renton has long made this hilltop town a prime residential retreat for sports icons like Kam Chancellor, Steve Hutchinson, Nate Burleson, Marcus Trufant, Mack Strong, Gus Bradley, and Dan Quinn.

On scorching July weekends, Lake Boren Park turns into the ultimate neighborhood gathering spot, which means the main parking lot hits total capacity by mid-morning. The insider play is to park at one of the downtown commercial plazas near Newcastle Way, grab your supplies, and make the easy five-minute walk over to the park on foot rather than fighting for a stall.

For a classic midsummer outing, grab an ice-cold craft pint and a slice on the outdoor patio at Resonate Brewery + Pizzeria on 119th Ave SE. When you are finished, stroll down to Lake Boren Park to walk out onto the fishing pier, where you can watch waterfowl and anglers casting into the clear water under the bright sky.

Timing is everything if you want to catch the best sunset views in the city. Head up to the public terrace at The Golf Club at Newcastle about 45 minutes before sunset to secure a view bench before the evening crowds arrive at The Calcutta Grill terrace bar. Watching the sky turn orange over Downtown Seattle and Mercer Island without paying a single dollar for admission is an unbeatable local ritual.

### **EP8**

August arrives with late-summer golden hour light and a soft haze hanging over the ridges. It is a time of slow afternoons and long, dry evenings. Looking out across the hillside homes today, it is wild to think this entire community almost went by a completely different name, as residents originally voted for Newport Hills before officially adopting Newcastle on September 30, 1994, to honor the pioneer coal mining operations that put this area on the map.

Managing daily errands in August heat means sticking to the shaded pockets of town. Running errands at the downtown QFC plaza is quick and easy, but if you need a break from the sun, the air-conditioned Newcastle Library on 129th Ave SE offers a cool space with free community programs and quiet reading nooks.

Start a hot August afternoon by picking up iced drinks from Ginkgo Coffee or a quick lunch from Tacos San Juan. Take your food down to Lake Boren Park and stake out a shady spot beneath one of the covered picnic pavilions or under the tall trees near the lawn, enjoying the gentle lake breezes.

When the sun starts to dip, take a cool retreat into the forested canyon trails of the Coal Creek Natural Area. The deep forest canopy keeps the trail significantly cooler than the open streets above. Walking across the wooden footbridges along Coal Creek while the afternoon light filters through the cedar branches is a perfect, quiet way to close out a hot late-summer day.

### **EP9**

September brings a sharp autumn clarity to the air, warm afternoons, and the biggest community gathering of the entire year. The headline event is Newcastle Days, kicking off around September 5 at Lake Boren Park, complete with a local parade, the Cars on the Green auto show, live music performance stages, and a massive lineup of regional food trucks celebrating the town's shared spirit.

With festival crowds flooding into town for Newcastle Days, traffic around Lake Boren and Coal Creek Parkway SE gets tight fast. Locals know to skip the main park entrance entirely and park north near downtown shopping centers or use side streets off 116th Ave SE, walking in to avoid getting stuck in post-parade traffic jams.

Take a break from the festival grounds by picking up a casual lunch at Cloud 9 Burgers or grabbing a specialty drink at Newcastle Coffeeroasters Spotlight in downtown Newcastle. Take your food down to the paved lakeside paths at Lake Boren Park to eat while taking in the festive sights, watching vintage cars line up on the grass under sharp September sunlight.

While enjoying the music and festivities, take a moment to reflect on how far this community has come since the 1870s, when the Seattle and Walla Walla Railroad was first laid down to carry Newcastle coal to Elliott Bay. What started as a rugged extraction camp built by immigrant miners from Wales, Italy, and China is now a tight-knit hilltop city celebrating its unique history right on the shores of Lake Boren.

### **EP10**

October brings brilliant autumn foliage to Newcastle, turning the trees along Coal Creek Parkway SE and the surrounding ravines into bright shades of yellow, amber, and orange. Crisp morning air and early dusk mark the true arrival of fall in the Pacific Northwest. This atmospheric season is a great time to reflect on the Coast Salish heritage of the land, where Snoqualmie, Duwamish, and Muckleshoot communities harvested berries and fished these watersheds for generations before industrial logging and mining took hold.

Autumn rain showers make driving on the steep neighborhood inclines around Newcastle a bit slick, so keeping your vehicle tires in good shape is essential. Commuter traffic on Coal Creek Parkway SE during rainy weekday rush hours slows down considerably, making back routes like SE May Valley Road or 116th Ave SE even more crucial for getting to Renton or Bellevue without constant brake lights.

For an ideal autumn morning, grab a warm drink from Starbucks or Ginkgo Coffee on 119th Ave SE, then take a drive to the Coal Creek Regional Park trailhead. Spend an hour walking the damp forest paths, admiring the colorful autumn leaf canopy, historic coal mine openings, and old rail bed structures covered in fresh rain moss.

Warm up after your trail walk with a hot bowl of soup or a meal at Yea's Wok or a warm pizza at Resonate Brewery + Pizzeria. Sitting in a comfortable booth while watching the rain hit the window glass is the ultimate October comfort ritual in Newcastle.

### **EP11**

November marks the arrival of heavy winter rains and early darkness, with the sun setting before 4:30 PM. The sound of rain drumming on tin roofs and deep forest leaves creates a cozy, enclosed atmosphere across the hilltop. It is during these dark, wet weeks that Newcastle's history feels most grounded, recalling the pioneer coal miners who worked deep underground beneath these exact hills to fuel early West Coast cities.

Running errands in November is all about quick efficiency. The downtown commercial hub at Coal Creek Parkway and Newcastle Way lets you hit QFC for groceries, stop by the bakery, and pick up dinner without having to make multiple stops across town. The flat plaza parking makes stepping out of the rain painless compared to navigating the slippery, steep residential side streets.

On a stormy afternoon, head up to the view overlook at The Golf Club at Newcastle on Six Penny Lane. Standing on the public terrace wrapped in a heavy coat to watch dark storm clouds sweep across Lake Washington and Downtown Seattle is a dramatic, zero-cost Pacific Northwest experience you will not soon forget.

Afterward, retreat indoors to the Newcastle Library on 129th Ave SE or grab a hot drink at Newcastle Coffeeroasters Spotlight. These indoor sanctuaries offer a warm, inclusive environment where locals gather to read, work, and chat while winter rain streams down the windows outside.

### **EP12**

December brings festive holiday light displays throughout Newcastle's hillside neighborhoods, casting a warm glow over the misty winter nights. The community closes out the year with a strong sense of togetherness. Looking back at the city's journey since its incorporation on September 30, 1994, Newcastle has successfully balanced its origins as a 19th-century industrial mining hub with its modern identity as a peaceful residential sanctuary.

Kick off a December weekend outing by getting a holiday espresso or hot cocoa at Ginkgo Coffee on 119th Ave SE or Starbucks in downtown Newcastle. Take a slow walk around Lake Boren Park, where the winter air is crisp, the paved paths remain easy to walk, and cold water reflections glisten under holiday evening lights.

Holiday errand rushes can create temporary traffic bottlenecks near Coal Creek Parkway SE shopping centers. To keep your days smooth, aim for early morning grocery runs at QFC and use 116th Ave SE to navigate around commuter bottlenecks when heading out of town for holiday shopping.

End your winter walk with a cozy dinner at The Calcutta Grill or grab takeout tacos from Tacos San Juan. As the year draws to a close, standing on the high ridges looking down at the twinkling lights of Downtown Seattle and Lake Washington reminds you why living on this historic hilltop feels so special.
